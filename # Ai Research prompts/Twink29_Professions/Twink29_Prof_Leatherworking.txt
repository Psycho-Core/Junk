TWINK29 - PROFESSIONS - STEP 04 - LEATHERWORKING
Server: private Battle for Azeroth 8.3.7 build 35662, TrinityCore base.
Scope: what Leatherworking gives a level 29 battleground twink, Classic
skill line 165, capped at 225 for a character of that level.

1. WHAT THE PROFESSION IS AT 29
   The whole value is one item type: armour kits. They are permanent, they
   work on any armour class, and four of them fit on one character. There
   are no bombs, no flasks, no scrolls and no gems here, and every wearable
   piece the profession makes below 225 is levelling gear with no stats.
   Rank gates, same as the other Classic professions: apprentice at level 5,
   journeyman at level 10 and skill 50, expert at level 20 and skill 125,
   artisan at level 35 and skill 200. A 29 therefore reaches 225 but never
   the artisan rank, and never the Dragonscale, Elemental or Tribal
   specialisation (225 plus level 40).
   8.3.7 caveat, same as the other profession files: the Classic
   SkillLineAbility rows carry a minimum rank of 1, so the quoted skill is
   the Classic orange value. Named trainers and their stock are Classic.

2. THE ARMOUR KITS - THE REASON TO TAKE IT
   All five are used, not crafted, and all five go on chest, legs, hands
   or feet. One kit per item. Stack of 10, one second cooldown, consumed.

   2304 Light Armor Kit - +8 armour, no item level restriction.
        Use spell 2831. Craft spell 2152, Leatherworking 1.
        Reagents 1x Light Leather 2318 (= 3x Ruined Leather Scraps 2934).
        Item level 5, no level requirement, sells for 15 copper.

   2313 Medium Armor Kit - +16 armour, no item level restriction stated.
        Use spell 2832. Craft spell 2165, Leatherworking 100, training
        cost 250, difficulty 100/115/122/130.
        Reagents 4x Medium Leather 2319 + 1x Coarse Thread 2320.
        Item level 15, requires level 5 to use, sells for 2 copper.

   4265 Heavy Armor Kit - +24 armour, only on items level 15 and above.
        Use spell 2833. Craft spell 3780, Leatherworking 150, training
        cost 750, difficulty 150/170/180/190.
        Reagents 5x Heavy Leather 4234 + 1x Fine Thread 2321.
        Item level 30, requires level 20 to use, sells for 6 silver 50.
        This is the best kit a 29 can apply. Four of them are +96 armour
        on a character whose whole gear set is around a thousand.

   8173 Thick Armor Kit - +32 armour, only on items level 25 and above.
        Use spell 10344. Craft spell 10487, Leatherworking 200.
        Reagents 5x Thick Leather 4304 + 1x Silken Thread 4291.
        Item level 40, requires level 30 to use. ALT - craftable at 29,
        unusable at 29, one level short.

   15564 Rugged Armor Kit - +40 armour, only on items level 35 and above.
        Use spell 19057. Craft spell 19058, Leatherworking 250.
        Reagent count unverified, the guide quotes 5x Rugged Leather 8170.
        Item level 50, requires level 40 to use. ALT on both counts,
        the skill is past the 225 cap and the level is past 29.

   Practical note: kits are ordinary trade goods, not soulbound. A twink
   buys four Heavy Armor Kits on the auction house for a few gold and never
   trains the profession. Levelling Leatherworking is for the crafter who
   wants to supply a whole roster, not for the twink itself.

3. LEG ARMOUR
   The leg slot takes the same armour kit as the chest, so the honest
   answer is +24 on the legs from a Heavy Armor Kit 4265, and that is the
   entire leg armour market at this tier.
   There is no separate leg-armour kit in Classic Leatherworking that a 29
   can use. The leg armour items people remember - the ones that add attack
   power or spell power to a leg slot - are Outland and Northrend patterns.
   Their skill requirement is far past 225 and their item level requirement
   is 60 or above, so they are ALT: they need a higher-level crafter and a
   higher-level wearer. Treat any such item as out of scope for this
   server's 29 bracket, and note that this file did not open their pages.

6. THE CHEAPEST ROUTE TO THE SKILL A 29 ACTUALLY NEEDS
   A 29 needs 150. That is the Heavy Armor Kit. Everything past 150 buys
   him nothing he can use, so the route below is split at that line.

   Refining, all trainer-taught and free:
     spell 2881  Light Leather  1x  = 3x Ruined Leather Scraps 2934
     spell 20648 Medium Leather 1x  = 4x Light Leather 2318
     spell 20649 Heavy Leather  1x  = 5x Medium Leather 2319
     spell 20650 Thick Leather  1x  = 6x Heavy Leather 4234
     Cured Light Hide 4231: 1x Light Hide 783 + 1x Salt 4289, spell 3816.
     Cured Heavy Hide 4236: spell 3818, 1x Heavy Hide 4235 + 3x Salt 4289.
       The ratio is taken from the Classic guide's shopping list, not from
       the spell page.

   Step   Recipe                     Spell  Min  Crafts  Reagents each
   ------------------------------------------------------------------
   1-20   2881 Light Leather          2881    1     19    3 scraps
   20-45  2304 Light Armor Kit         2152    1     40    1 light leather
   45-55  7276 Handstitched Cloak      9058    1     10    2 LL, 1 coarse
   55-80  4239 Embossed Gloves          3756   55     25    3 LL, 2 coarse
   80-100 4246 Fine Leather Belt        3763   80     20    6 LL, 2 coarse
   100-130 2313 Medium Armor Kit        2165  100     30    4 medium, 1 coarse
   130-150 5961 Dark Leather Pants      7135  115     20    12 medium, 1 dye
                                                        and 1 fine thread
   150-175 4265 Heavy Armor Kit         3780  150     25    5 heavy, 1 fine
   175-190 5964 Barbaric Shoulders      7151  175     15    8 heavy, 1 cured
                                                        heavy, 2 fine
   190-200 5966 Guardian Gloves         7156  190     10    4 heavy, 1 cured
                                                        heavy, 1 silken
   200-225 8173 Thick Armor Kit        10487  200     25    5 thick, 1 silken

   Every step is trainer-taught. The 100-130 medium kits are yellow from
   115 and the 130-150 pants are yellow from 140, so the last few points
   of each block may need extra crafts; the counts assume average luck.

   Shopping list to 150, which is the twink stop:
     57x Ruined Leather Scraps 2934  (makes 19 Light Leather)
     255x Light Leather 2318
     130x Coarse Thread 2320
     360x Medium Leather 2319
     20x Fine Thread 2321
     20x Gray Dye 4340
   To go on to 175 add 125x Heavy Leather 4234 and 25x Fine Thread.
   To reach 225 add, cumulatively, 285x Heavy Leather 4234, 25x Heavy Hide
   4235 and 75x Salt 4289 for the curing, 55 more Fine Thread, 35x Silken
   Thread 4291, and 125x Thick Leather 4304 - which is 750 Heavy Leather
   once refined, the single most expensive stretch in the profession.

   Where the materials come from: everything from 2934 up to 4304 is
   skinning loot off beasts of the matching band - scraps and light leather
   from levels 1-20 beasts, medium 15-30, heavy 30-40, thick 40-50 - or the
   auction house. Light Hide 783 and Medium Hide 4232 are skinning as well
   and drop far less often than leather; Heavy Hide 4235 is the rarest of
   the four and is the bottleneck in the 175-200 stretch. Coarse Thread
   2320, Fine Thread 2321, Silken Thread 4291, Gray Dye 4340 and Salt 4289
   are sold by the trade supply vendors that stand next to every trainer,
   a few silver each. Leatherworking pairs with Skinning for exactly this
   reason; a 29 who skins his own mobs pays nothing for the leather.

   If the goal is only four Heavy Armor Kits for his own gear, he needs
   skill 150, five Heavy Leather per kit, and no recipe hunting at all -
   every step above is trainer-taught except the Fine Leather Gloves
   pattern 2408, which this route does not use.

7. ALT - WHAT A 29 CANNOT DO ALONE
   8173 Thick Armor Kit - craftable at 200, usable only at level 30.
   15564 Rugged Armor Kit - skill 250, past the cap; usable only at level 40.
   2408 Pattern: Fine Leather Gloves - a drop or a pickpocket, so the
     gloves at 2312 are ALT for a self-sufficient 29 without the pattern.
   5964 Barbaric Shoulders - wearable only at level 30.
   5966 Guardian Gloves - wearable only at level 33.
   Telonis 4212 and Una 3007 teach to 225; anything past that, including
     the dragonscale, elemental and tribal specialisations, needs the
     artisan rank at level 35, so it is ALT by definition.
   The Outland and Northrend leg-armour kits, and every mail piece the
     profession makes for a level 40 or better wearer, are ALT as well.
   A higher-level crafter is also the answer for the Thick and Rugged kits
     if a 29 roster wants them on 39s and 49s on the same account.

8. WHICH CLASSES SHOULD TAKE IT
   Armour kits are the only item in the game's crafting professions at this
   tier that work on cloth, leather and mail alike, so this is the one
   profession whose product every class can use. That does not make it a
   good profession for every class - it makes it a good purchase.

   1. Rogue. Leather, melee, takes the most physical hits of the leather
      wearers, and four Heavy Kits are the cheapest armour he will ever
      buy. Still second choice behind engineering for the damage, first
      choice if the server's bracket is melee-heavy.
   2. Druid. Same kits, and a bear-form armour multiplier turns flat item
      armour into more than 96 - the multiplier value unverified, the
      mechanic is standard.
   3. Warrior and paladin. Both wear leather at 29, so all four kit slots
      work; the profession gives them nothing else at this level, and
      blacksmithing covers the weapon side.
   4. Hunter and shaman. Leather at 29, mail at 40. Kits now, nothing later
      that this skill band can make.
   5. Priest, mage, warlock. Kits work on cloth, but armour is the wrong
      stat to spend a profession slot on. Tailoring and enchanting beat it
      outright.
   Last line: nobody should level Leatherworking for a 29. Buy four Heavy
   Armor Kits 4265 off the auction house, put them on chest, legs, hands
   and feet, and spend the profession slot on engineering.
