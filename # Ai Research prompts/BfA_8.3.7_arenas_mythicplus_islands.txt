1.2  TEAM SIZES AND QUEUE MODES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Rated arena: 2v2 and 3v3 only. The 5v5 bracket was retired in patch 7.0.3 [W], so a 8.3.7
  server has exactly two rated brackets and no third.
  Skirmish (unrated): 2v2 and 3v3, open from level 15, brackets every 10 levels, solo or partial
  groups are filled from the queue [W]. A level-120 skirmish therefore uses bracket 120.
  War Games: an arena started against chosen opponents; it does not change any rating [W].
  The client table does not carry the bracket size. BattlemasterList min/max player columns read
  0 and 5 for arena rows and are not the team size; do not wire them as 2 or 3.

1.3  SPAWN POINTS, PILLARS AND LINE OF SIGHT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Coordinates: UNVERIFIED here, but the exact lookup path is known. TrinityCore does not store
  arena spawns in a DB2 of its own. BattlegroundMgr::LoadBattlegroundTemplates runs
  "SELECT ID, MinPlayersPerTeam, MaxPlayersPerTeam, MinLvl, MaxLvl, AllianceStartLoc,
  AllianceStartO, HordeStartLoc, HordeStartO, StartMaxDist, Weight, ScriptName
  FROM battleground_template" and resolves each StartLoc id through the WorldSafeLocs store
  (source: TrinityCore BattlegroundMgr.cpp, LoadBattlegroundTemplates). CreateNewBattleground
  then calls SetTeamStartPosition for both teams, and GetTeamStartPosition is what a join uses,
  so those two WorldSafeLocs rows are the literal spawn points. The wago.tools 8.3.7 export has
  no WorldSafeLocs table, so this document cannot print the values - run this on your server:

    SELECT ID, AllianceStartLoc, AllianceStartO, HordeStartLoc, HordeStartO, StartMaxDist
    FROM battleground_template ORDER BY ID;

  The ids that come back are WorldSafeLocs entries; resolve them in your own extracted
  WorldSafeLocs (dbc/db2) or take the coordinates straight off the arena map files. Which rows
  your core actually populated, and whether each arena has its own row or shares a generic one,
  is core-specific: unverified from here.

  Match start timing is verifiable and identical for all twelve maps: Arena.cpp sets the four
  start events to BG_START_DELAY_1M, 30 s, 15 s and 0, so a 60-second preparation countdown
  runs before the gates open (source: TrinityCore Arena.cpp, Arena::Arena). A companion should
  treat those 60 seconds as the positioning window described in 1.5.

  Pillars and line-of-sight objects are map geometry, not table rows: they live in the WMO/ADT
  files of the map. No DB2 in build 35662 lists per-arena pillar positions, so any count or
  coordinate printed here would be invented. Two practical routes, both server-side:
    - open the arena map in a WMO/ADT viewer and record the obstacle outlines per map id;
    - check whether your core spawns any arena obstacles as game objects:
        SELECT guid, id, map, position_x, position_y, position_z FROM gameobject
        WHERE map IN (1672,1911,1504,1505,572,617,980,1134,1552,1170,1825,2167);
      Anything that returns is a real, citable position; an empty result means the obstacles
      are pure map geometry and only the viewer route is left.
  Pillar layout per arena: unverified in this document. Behaviour that does not need the
  geometry is in 1.6, and the generic line-of-sight rules a companion can execute are in 1.5.

1.5  FIRST 30 SECONDS AND MID-MATCH, BY ROLE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  These are operating procedures derived from the mechanics in 1.6, not client facts. They are
  written so a companion state machine can execute them without judgement calls.

  TANK (any tank specialisation)
    First 30 s: hold the enemy healer or the highest-damage caster in melee, keep the enemy team
      between you and your healer, and do not use a gap closer in the first global - the opener is
      a trade of crowd control, and a wasted charge is a wasted trinket later. Face the target
      away from your own healer so a cleave cannot hit both.
    Mid-match: stay on the target your damage dealers are on (a tank that splits damage is a tank
      that gives the enemy healer free time), peel with a slow or a root when your healer is
      trained, and hold your defensive cooldown for the enemy damage burst, not for the opener.
  HEALER
    First 30 s: open with the cheapest heal that keeps everyone above 70%, stand at maximum
      range behind a pillar, and pre-position so that line of sight to all three team-mates is
      possible from one spot. Do not trinket the first crowd control unless it is on the kill
      target - the second one is the one that matters.
    Mid-match: heal through the burst, then drink or regenerate in every gap; call the
      dispel target before the second stack lands, and swap position after every trinket use.
  DAMAGE
    First 30 s: one damage dealer opens on the kill target, the other controls the enemy healer.
      Do not break your own crowd control with a cleave - a companion must check the crowd
      control aura on its focus target before every area attack.
    Mid-match: swap to the target the enemy healer cannot reach, keep one interrupt reserved for
      the enemy healer's cast, and hold the offensive cooldown for the moment the enemy trinket
      is down (2-minute cooldown, 1.6).

1.6  CROWD CONTROL AND TRINKET RULES THAT THE DATA SUPPORTS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  spell id | name                   | cd    | what it does (Wowhead tooltip)
  --------+------------------------+-------+---------------------------------------
  208683   | Gladiator's Medallion  | 2 min | removes movement-impairing effects and
           |                        |       | all effects causing loss of control,
           |                        |       | in PvP combat
  42292    | PvP Trinket            | 2 min | same effect; older id, still in SpellName
  65547    | PvP Trinket            | 2 min | same effect; rank variant

  Rules a companion has to encode [H][W]:
    - The trinket has a 2-minute cooldown [H]. It is not usable twice inside one burst window,
      so the AI must treat "trinket available" as a resource, not as a reflex.
    - It removes loss-of-control and movement-impairing effects; it does not remove silences,
      disarms or damage-over-time effects [H].
    - Diminishing returns, from the wiki page "Diminishing returns" [W]: the first control
      effect of a category on a target runs at full PvP duration; a second of the same category
      inside the window is cut 50%, a third 75%, and the target is then immune to that category
      until the window expires. The page states the window as 18 seconds, measured from the end
      of the previous effect; its own patch history shows that number has been retuned since,
      so 18 s is the documented figure for 8.3.7, not a client field - confirm it in your core.
    - Categories, from the wiki page "Crowd control" [W]: since Warlords of Draenor there are six
      diminishing-returns types - Roots, Stuns, Incapacitates (which covers Polymorph, Sap, Charm
      and Sleep), Disorients (which covers Fear, Horror and Cyclone), Silences, and area
      knockbacks. Effects inside one type share the counter; effects in different types do not.
      So fear and polymorph do NOT share a counter, but fear and horror do. A companion should
      keep one type per target in flight and must not spend its trinket on the first stun of a
      sequence.
    - The per-type maximum PvP duration for 8.3.7 is still unverified: neither the crowd-control
      page nor the diminishing-returns page states a BfA-era cap, and no client table carries
      one.

1.7  DAMPENING (VERIFIED FIGURES)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  From warcraft.wiki.gg "Arena" [W]:
    - Dampening is a stacking debuff that reduces healing received and absorption by 1% per stack.
    - It is applied after 5 minutes.
    - In 2v2 matches in which each side has at least one healer, Dampening starts instantly.
    - After it starts, every player gains a stack every 10 seconds.
    - A match that runs 25 minutes ends in a draw.
    - Necrotic Strike's healing absorption is not affected by Dampening.
  The client does carry a Dampening spell: id 110310, name "Dampening", tooltip "Reduces healing
  and absorption effects." [C][H]. It is a marker aura - the 1%-per-stack value, the 10-second
  step and the 5-minute start are not in any field of that row, so the numbers above stay
  wiki-verified and the implementation stays server-side. 74411 "Battleground - Dampening" is
  the separate battleground marker and is not the arena aura.
  Companion consequence: a healer AI must switch from "keep everyone topped" to "trade cooldowns"
  at the 5-minute mark, and a 2v2 healer mirror should expect dampening from the first global.

1.8  RATING GAIN AND LOSS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  No client table carries this, but the core does, and the core is what you run. In
  TrinityCore the arena rating change is not a single K-factor. From ArenaTeam.cpp [T]:

    chance   = 1 / (1 + 10 ^ ((opponentRating - ownRating) / 650))       [Elo, 650 scale]
    win, own rating < 1000   : mod = W1 * (1 - chance)
    win, own rating 1000-1300: mod = (W1/2 + (W1/2)*((1300-own)/300)) * (1 - chance)
    win, own rating >= 1300  : mod = W2 * (1 - chance)
    loss                     : mod = L * (-chance)
    every result is rounded up (ceil), not truncated
    MMR change               : mod = ((won ? 1 : 0) - chance) * MMRmod

  W1, W2, L and MMRmod are worldserver.conf floats, read through the config ids
  CONFIG_ARENA_WIN_RATING_MODIFIER_1, _2, CONFIG_ARENA_LOSE_RATING_MODIFIER and
  CONFIG_ARENA_MATCHMAKER_RATING_MODIFIER [T]. So the "K" a companion has to know is whatever
  your worldserver.conf sets - print it there:

    grep -i 'Arena\.\(Rating\|Matchmaker\)' worldserver.conf

  Two structural points that follow from the formula and are worth encoding: the change depends
  on the opponent's rating through the 650-scale chance, so beating a much lower-rated team
  moves the number by very little, and losing to one costs close to the full loss modifier; and
  below 1300 the win modifier is halved and scaled again, which is why low-rated teams climb
  faster per win.
  Still unverified for 8.3.7 specifically: the retail personal-rating numbers. TrinityCore's
  model above is the 3.3.5 team-rating implementation; a BfA-era core tracks personal rating in
  a different class, but the same three modifiers plus the MMR modifier are the knobs. Skirmish
  and War Games change neither rating [W].

    level 2  : first affix, always one of two, alternating week by week:
               Fortified 10 or Tyrannical 9.
    level 4  : second affix, from a fixed list of five:
               Bolstering 7, Bursting 11, Raging 6, Sanguine 8, Teeming 5.
    level 7  : third affix, from a fixed list of six:
               Explosive 13, Grievous 12, Necrotic 4, Quaking 14, Skittish 2, Volcanic 3.
    level 10 : fourth affix = the season affix, fixed for the whole season. Season 4 =
               Awakened 120. Level 10+ keys therefore carry four affixes at once.
  The same page records that the week-to-week affixes are set game-wide and change only at the
  weekly reset, and that the seasonal affix does not rotate [W].

  The actual Season 4 cycle is a 12-week loop, and it was published weekly by mythicpl.us. The
  archived page from 2020-06-09 carries the finished table in cycle order (the site's own
  next-week logic walks that table row by row and wraps at 12) [A]:

  Read it as a rotation, not a calendar: the table fixes the order of the twelve weeks, and the
  site maps a week number onto it from the live API. Which row was "week 1" of Season 4 is
  unverified here - the archived page no longer carries the highlighted row, because that
  highlight was applied at run time from raider.io. What is verified is the set and the order:
  six Fortified weeks alternating with six Tyrannical weeks, every level-4 affix paired with a
  different level-7 affix, and no pair repeating inside one loop. The February 2020 snapshot of
  the same page still had a broken table (two Tyrannical weeks back to back, no Teeming/Skittish
  pair); the June table is the corrected one, which is why the June snapshot is the one copied.
  If your server wants a calendar rather than a cycle, derive it: take the date of the first
  Season 4 reset your realm ran and add seven days per row.

2.5  WHAT EACH ROLE DOES AGAINST EACH AFFIX
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Written against the client text in 2.2, so every stated effect is the same one the client
  shows. Actions are procedures, not client facts.

  Fortified (10) - non-boss enemies +20% health, up to +30% damage
    tank  : pull one pack at a time and never chain-pull two; count forced percentage before the
            last boss.
    healer: trash damage is the whole fight: keep the tank above 70% and pre-heal the pull, not
            the hit.
    damage: kill the highest-forces creature first (2.4); area damage only after the pack is
            gathered.

  Tyrannical (9) - bosses +40% health, up to +15% damage
    tank  : hold every boss cooldown for the phase the group has failed before; call the swap
            timer aloud.
    healer: boss damage is spiky: bank the big cooldown for the one ability that can delete a
            tank.
    damage: a single-target build and a single-target trinket for boss weeks; area damage is
            wasted here.

  Bolstering (7) - a dying non-boss empowers nearby allies (+health, +damage)
    tank  : never let a low-health pack member die next to a full-health one: drag the pack apart
            first.
    healer: expect a damage spike each time something dies; heal after the kill, not during it.
    damage: kill the smallest-health creature away from the pack, or kill everything at once.

  Bursting (11) - dying non-boss explodes for 14% max health over 4 s, stacking
    tank  : stagger kills; stop damage at 10% and let the stack fall off before the next kill.
    healer: this is a healing-throughput affix: stack heals before the third kill, dispel nothing
            here.
    damage: never kill two things in the same global; track the stack and hold area damage.

  Sanguine (8) - corpses leave ichor that heals enemies and damages players
    tank  : move the pack off the pool after every kill; a tank standing in ichor out-heals the
            group.
    healer: the pool is not healable damage - the group has to move, so move with them.
    damage: kill, then step: never area-damage a pack that is standing in its own pool.

  Necrotic (4) - melee stacks a blight that reduces healing received
    tank  : kite once the stacks outpace healing (a dozen or so is the usual call, not a client
            value).
    healer: the tank becomes unhealable at high stacks: call the kite before it is critical.
    damage: slow and root the pack to let the tank kite; do not chase into the group.

  Skittish (2) - enemies pay far less attention to threat from tanks
    tank  : re-establish threat on every pull; assume the first global of area damage will rip a
            mob.
    healer: expect to heal damage dealers; the affix is a threat problem that shows up as healing.
    damage: wait two globals before opening, and pull back to the tank if a mob turns.

  Quaking (14) - players periodically emit a shockwave that interrupts nearby allies
    tank  : spread before the cast; the tank is the only unit that can stay in melee during it.
    healer: the shockwave interrupts casts: never start a long heal in the last second of the
            cycle.
    damage: spread and stop casting; a caster caught in it loses the cast and the school for a
            moment.

  Explosive (13) - enemies periodically summon Explosive Orbs that detonate if not killed
    tank  : hold the pack still so the orbs spawn in reach; do not chase them yourself.
    healer: orbs are the group's job and they explode for group damage: track how many are alive.
    damage: assign one damage dealer to orbs for the whole dungeon; swap instantly when one
            spawns.

  Grievous (12) - below 90% health, players take increasing damage until healed above 90%
    tank  : the tank is the worst case: never drop below 90% on purpose to save a cooldown.
    healer: this is a top-up affix: heal everyone to 90% before starting the next cast, no
            exceptions.
    damage: do not stand in avoidable damage; any chip damage becomes a healing debt.

  Volcanic (3) - gouts of flame erupt under distant players
    tank  : melee is safe; the tank should not move the pack for a gout that spawned on a ranged
            unit.
    healer: move out of the gout and keep casting; the gout does not follow.
    damage: ranged spread; the gout targets distance, so a clumped ranged group eats several at
            once.

  Raging (6) - non-boss enrages at 30% health for +100% damage
    tank  : hold a defensive for the 30% moment on every pack; kill the enraged mob first.
    healer: expect a step change in damage at 30%: pre-heal the tank before the pack crosses it.
    damage: burst the last 30%: a slow kill on a Raging pack is the most common way to lose the
            tank.

  Teeming (5) - additional non-boss enemies are present throughout the dungeon
    tank  : the routes change: more packs, so count percentage before pulling anything optional.
    healer: more pulls means more total damage: mana is the resource to protect.
    damage: kill order matters more, because there are more mobs competing for the same area
            damage.

  Awakened (120) - obelisks let players enter Ny'alotha and fight servants of N'Zoth; a servant
  not dealt with appears during the final boss
    tank  : decide before the run which obelisks the group clears; a servant left alive is a boss-
            phase add.
    healer: the obelisk phase is a burst check: cooldowns are better spent there than on trash.
    damage: clear the servant inside the obelisk, never leave it for the last boss unless the
            timer needs it.

2.6  PULLS THAT ARE SKIPPED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  UNVERIFIED as a fixed list. Which packs are skipped is a route decision made per run: it
  depends on the keystone level, the affix set (Teeming adds packs; Bolstering and Sanguine make
  small packs expensive), and the group's damage. MDT ships the pack coordinates (2.4) but the
  routes themselves live in saved variables, not in the addon, so there is no shipped list of
  skips to verify. The only arithmetic that is verifiable is the one in 2.1: a dungeon needs
  exactly "forces" worth of kills, so any pack that is skipped has to be paid for elsewhere.

3.2  OBJECTIVES AND AZERITE TARGETS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  [W] Every island has at least four start locations, and the queue uses one of them, so the
  opening move differs run to run. Islands carry azerite nodes and chests, capture points,
  quest givers, consumable and shrine objects, caves and ships, and randomised denizens; the
  same island can be populated by completely different mob factions on two visits.
  Victory is a race: the team that reaches the required azerite total first wins [B]. What
  happens when the match ends with neither team at the total is not stated on the revision I
  read - most azerite wins is the obvious reading, but it is unverified, so a companion should
  keep gathering until the result screen rather than stopping on the opponent's lead.

  The totals themselves are published, though not on the wiki page:

    mode   | azerite to win | item level | azerite gained | enemy health | source
    -------+----------------+------------+----------------+--------------+--------
    Normal |          6,000 | none       | 100%           | 100%         | [B][G]
    Heroic |          9,000 | 295        | 150%           | 200%         | [G]
    Mythic |         12,000 | 315        | 200%           | 300%         | [G]
    PvP    |         12,000 | none       | 200%           | 300%         | [G]

  Blizzard's own preview states the 6,000 figure for the mode it describes [B]; the 9,000 and
  12,000 cells and the three multiplier columns come from the guide table on Icy Veins [G] and
  are repeated identically by several other guide sites, but no client table and no wiki
  revision I could reach carries them. Treat 9,000 and 12,000 as well-attested secondary data
  and 6,000 as primary. The % columns are relative to Normal.
  Companion arithmetic that follows: a Normal run needs 6,000 azerite, a Mythic run 12,000 but
  every pickup is worth double, so the number of pickups to win is roughly the same in both -
  what changes is the enemy health, not the route length.

  Azerite node positions are not in the client tables - the scenario script places them - so
  node coordinates are unverified. Only the start locations are documented, and only as a
  count (at least four per island), never as coordinates [W].
  Match length: the wiki states expeditions "take around 15-20 minutes to complete" [W]; with
  the totals above, a group that wins on Normal has pulled 6,000 azerite in that window, i.e.
  roughly 300-400 azerite a minute, which is the rate a companion's route has to beat.

3.5  WHAT A COMPANION SHOULD DO ON AN ISLAND
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Procedures, derived from 3.2-3.4:
    1. At the start, pick the nearest azerite cluster from the start location the queue gave;
       the start location is one of four and is not fixed, so the route must be selected at
       runtime, not hard-coded.
    2. Never fight a full-health island pack for a single node: island mobs are the clock.
    3. Keep one unit on the enemy-team side of the map to report contest timing, and only
       commit the group when the enemy team is already in combat.
    4. Dubloons and cosmetics are per-run, not per-kill since 8.1.0; there is no reason to
       clear a pack that does not sit on azerite.

4.3  STILL UNVERIFIED, WITH THE REASON
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Arena spawn COORDINATES: the StartLoc ids are known but resolve through WorldSafeLocs, and
    that table is not in the wago.tools export under any of the four spellings I tried, on
    8.3.7, 9.0.2 or 11.0.2. Extract it from your own client or read it from your world DB.
  - Pillar positions as coordinates: map geometry, not DB2. Layout prose is in 1.3; positions
    would need a WMO/ADT viewer.
  - Retail personal-rating numbers for 8.3.7: the core formula is in 1.8, but the retail
    per-win values are server configuration in every core I can read.
  - Per-category maximum PvP duration for 8.3.7: neither wiki page states a BfA-era cap.
  - Which calendar week was week 1 of the Season 4 cycle: the archived page highlighted the
    current row at run time from raider.io, so the highlight is not in the archive.
  - Which pulls a route skips: route data lives in per-player MDT saved variables and in
    raider.io's weekly route strings, which are addon-encoded blobs, not tables.
  - Freehold and The Underrot teeming totals: MDT declares them lower than normal (see 2.1).
  - Island azerite node coordinates: placed by the scenario script, not in any table.
  - Island dubloon payout per run and per-difficulty drop rates: not published in anything I
    could read. The salvage PRICES in 3.4 are vendor prices and are verified.
  - Storming: absent from KeystoneAffix and from the BfA tier lists, recorded so a companion
    ported from a Legion build does not go looking for it.
