1. THE SHORT ANSWER
===================

1.1  Retail 8.3.7 has one loot method: personal loot
----------------------------------------------------
Patch 8.0.1 (2018-07-17) removed the ability to change loot modes. The patch note quoted on the
wiki's Loot article reads: "The ability to change loot modes has been removed. Personal loot is
now the sole option in all situations." The Master looter article carries the same removal
banner (patch 8.0.1). Group loot, need before greed, free for all, round robin and master loot
are therefore pre-8.0.1 methods: an 8.3.7 client cannot select any of them.
Consequence for a retail-accurate 8.3.7 server: a roll window never appears. There is no
need/greed/disenchant/pass prompt, no roll timer and no 1-100 roll, because the only method that
produces them is gone. Sections 5 to 8 describe those mechanics anyway, because your server can
still run them (1.2) and because a companion has to answer them correctly when it does.

1.2  Your server still compiles all six methods
-----------------------------------------------
TrinityCore's Loot.h keeps the whole client enum, and the loot generator still branches on it.
GROUP_LOOT and NEED_BEFORE_GREED are fully implemented and produce a real roll window in the
8.3.7 client; MASTER_LOOT is implemented too, including the assign-to-player packet.

| Value | Enum              | State in TrinityCore                                |
|-------|-------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| 0     | FREE_FOR_ALL      | implemented                                         |
| 1     | ROUND_ROBIN       | implemented                                         |
| 2     | MASTER_LOOT       | implemented (CMSG_MASTER_LOOT_ITEM)                 |
| 3     | GROUP_LOOT        | implemented, rolls every item at or above threshold |
| 4     | NEED_BEFORE_GREED | implemented, Need is per-player filtered            |
| 5     | PERSONAL_LOOT     | implemented, default for every new group            |

The values above are the bytes TrinityCore writes into the StartLootRoll packet and the group
update block, so they are also the values the 8.3.7 client interprets as loot method.
Two server-side facts decide what a companion will actually see:

  - A new TrinityCore group is created with lootMethod = PERSONAL_LOOT and lootThreshold =
    ITEM_QUALITY_UNCOMMON (value 2). Both are stored per group in the characters.group table
    (columns lootMethod and lootThreshold), so they survive restarts and can be edited there.
  - WorldSession::HandleSetLootMethodOpcode in the current source is commented out under the
    comment "not allowed to change" - the client cannot change the method. On an 8.3.7 fork that
    still has the old handler the leader could set FREE_FOR_ALL, MASTER_LOOT, GROUP_LOOT or
    PERSONAL_LOOT (NEED_BEFORE_GREED was not in that whitelist), never inside an LFG group.
So: the loot method on your server is a server decision - a group DB row, a script call to
Group::SetLootMethod, or a custom command - not a player decision. Everything a companion sees
is downstream of that decision.

1.3  The three ways loot is shared in 8.3.7
-------------------------------------------

| Mechanism     | Applies to                    | Result                     |
|---------------|-------------------------------|----------------------------|
| Personal loot | every instance and world boss | the player the game picked |
| Legacy loot   | content 11+ levels below you  | first to loot the corpse   |
| Group trade   | BoP drops, 2 hours            | another eligible member    |

There is no fourth way. Nothing in 8.3.7 hands a group member the right to distribute another
player's loot, and nothing lets a player roll against another player for a drop.

2. PERSONAL LOOT - THE ONLY METHOD RETAIL 8.3.7 HAS
===================================================

2.1  What it does
-----------------

  - The game picks a number of players based on group size and gives each of them a random item
    for their loot specialization; everyone else receives gold specific to them (wiki Loot, 2020
    revision).
  - Everyone still loots the boss corpse themselves. The corpse shows each player only their own
    assignment (TrinityCore marks those slots LOOT_SLOT_TYPE_OWNER = 4), and the chat log
    announces who won what.
  - Mounts can be awarded this way in addition to the normal item roll (wiki).
  - Each player in a dungeon group always receives an item from the final dungeon boss (hotfix
    2014-11-21, still listed as current in the 2020 Loot article).
  - A solo player killing a current raid boss gets the single-player share of the same ratio -
    about a 20% chance of one loot-spec-eligible drop. Legacy loot replaces this with a full
    group's worth of drops (modern wiki Loot article; see section 3).

2.2  How many items a boss awards
---------------------------------
The count is derived from the number of eligible players. The ratio most often quoted - and the
one that fits the community measurements - is one item per five eligible players, i.e. 20% per
player. Blizzard never published the 8.3.7 coefficient, so treat the exact value as UNVERIFIED
and the table as the shape of the curve:

| Eligible players | Items            | Eligible players | Items |
|------------------|------------------|------------------|-------|
| 1                | 20% chance of 1  | 20               | 4     |
| 5                | 1                | 25               | 5     |
| 10               | 2                | 30               | 6     |
| 12               | 2 + 40% of a 3rd | 40               | 8     |

TrinityCore uses no 20% coefficient. Dungeon and raid encounter loot comes from
GenerateDungeonEncounterPersonalLoot (LootMgr.cpp): it rolls the boss loot table once and then
hands each resulting item to one randomly chosen eligible player
(LootTemplate::ProcessPersonalLoot). Ordinary creature loot takes the other path, Loot::FillLoot
with personal = true, which fills loot only for the player who opened the corpse. How many items
a boss yields on your server is therefore a property of its loot template, not of a group-size
formula.

2.3  What a personal-loot winner may do with the item
-----------------------------------------------------
Retail rule: a personal-loot item is tradeable to anyone in the group as long as it is not an
item-level upgrade for the winner (wiki Loot). Section 9 gives the full trade rule.
TrinityCore rule: the item is stored soulbound and is not tradeable at all, because the normal
loot path calls Player::StoreNewItem with an empty allowed-looter set (Loot::StoreLootItem), and
Item::SetSoulboundTradeable is only reached when that set holds more than one GUID. Only the
master-loot give path passes the real set (LootHandler::HandleLootMasterGiveOpcode). The API to
do it right exists - see 9.4.

2.4  Bonus rolls are personal loot too
--------------------------------------
The BfA bonus-roll token is the currency Seal of Wartorn Fate, currency id 1580 (CurrencyTypes,
build 35662; description: "Twists fate to provide an opportunity for additional treasure from
Battle for Azeroth raid and dungeon bosses"). A bonus roll is a second personal-loot draw on a
boss you already killed. Whether a bonus-rolled item can be traded is UNVERIFIED for 8.3.7.

3. LEGACY LOOT MODE
===================

3.1  The rule
-------------
Legacy Loot Mode was added in the 8.0.1 pre-patch. It switches on automatically when a player
enters an instance at least 11 levels above the maximum level of the content - Blizzard's own
wording is "more than 10 levels higher". The 2020 Loot article says "11 levels or more"; the
Patch 8.0.1 page still says "10 levels or more". The blue post of 2018-02-21 ("more than 10
levels") and the tests that followed put the threshold at 11: at 120 the last expansion that
qualifies is Warlords of Draenor.
Under legacy loot the boss drops loot as if a full group had killed it (Blizzard, "Loot Rules
for Transmog Runs", 2018-02-21):

  - the number of items equals what a full party would have received - a 5-player dungeon gives
    five players' worth;
  - flexible raids, including Raid Finder, behave as though 20 players were present;
  - every item on the loot table can drop, not only items flagged for your specialization.
The loot appears on the corpse as ordinary loot. There is no personal assignment, no roll
window, and no list of winners; whoever opens the corpse takes what is there. With several
players present the items are split among them and remain tradeable (same blue post).

3.2  What qualifies at level 120
--------------------------------

| Expansion              | Content max level | 120 minus max | Legacy at 120?         |
|------------------------|-------------------|---------------|------------------------|
| Classic                | 60                | 60            | yes                    |
| The Burning Crusade    | 70                | 50            | yes                    |
| Wrath of the Lich King | 80                | 40            | yes                    |
| Cataclysm              | 85                | 35            | yes                    |
| Mists of Pandaria      | 90                | 30            | yes                    |
| Warlords of Draenor    | 100               | 20            | yes                    |
| Legion                 | 110               | 10            | no - exactly 10 levels |
| Battle for Azeroth     | 120               | 0             | no                     |

The Legion row is the one that surprises people: at level 120 a Legion raid boss is exactly ten
levels below you, so it stays on personal loot and drops one spec item per boss at most. Legion
only became legacy after the Shadowlands level squish (cap 60, Legion content at 45).

3.3  TrinityCore does not implement it
--------------------------------------
TrinityCore has no legacy-loot branch: Loot.cpp, LootMgr.cpp and LootHandler.cpp never test for
it, Player.cpp knows only the ITEM_FLAG_LEGACY item flag, and the server opcode
SMSG_LEGACY_LOOT_RULES is declared STATUS_UNHANDLED and never sent. A stock TrinityCore 8.3.7
server therefore keeps personal loot in old raids. If you want the retail behaviour you have to
detect the level difference yourself, suppress the personal generation and call Loot::FillLoot
with personal = false so the whole table is rolled onto the corpse.

4. THE FIVE OLD METHODS AND WHAT IS LEFT OF THEM IN 8.3.7
=========================================================

Definitions below are the 2020 wiki's description of the pre-8.0.1 system. The "retail 8.3.7"
column answers the user-facing question; the "TrinityCore" column is what the server code does
if you select that value.

| # | Method            | Who loots the corpse        | Rolls? | Retail 8.3.7    | TrinityCore |
|---|-------------------|-----------------------------|--------|-----------------|-------------|
| 0 | Free for all      | any eligible member         | no     | removed 8.0.1   | works       |
| 1 | Round robin       | one member at a time        | no     | removed 8.0.1   | works       |
| 2 | Master loot       | the master looter only      | no     | removed 8.0.1   | works       |
| 3 | Group loot        | anyone, rolls item by item  | yes    | removed 8.0.1   | works       |
| 4 | Need before greed | anyone, rolls item by item  | yes    | removed 8.0.1   | works       |
| 5 | Personal loot     | each player loots own slots | no     | the only method | default     |

4.1  The definitions that matter
--------------------------------

  - Free for all: first come, first served; every eligible member may loot the whole corpse.
  - Round robin: members take turns; each corpse belongs to one member until the next kill.
  - Master loot: one designated player loots everything and assigns each item to a member;
    assigning a Bind-on-Pickup item asks for confirmation. The wiki described it as available to
    guild-group raids. Any loot on a corpse could only be taken by the player who was master
    looter at the moment of the kill - if the master looter left, the corpse was dead loot.
  - Group loot: like round robin, but every item at or above the loot threshold is rolled by the
    whole group. It was the default for all groups outside random dungeons.
  - Need before greed: like group loot, but a player who cannot use the item cannot press Need.
    It was the unalterable default for Dungeon Finder groups.
Master loot, group loot and need before greed all produced the same roll window described in
section 5; they differ only in who is offered which buttons and in what happens to items nobody
rolls on.

4.2  What the removal actually took away
----------------------------------------
Three things a companion could do before 8.0.1 and cannot do in 8.3.7: select a loot method
through the client (CMSG_SET_LOOT_METHOD is dead), be handed an item by a master looter, and
press a roll button on a drop. On a retail-accurate 8.3.7 server the only loot decision a
character makes is which of its own personal-loot items to take, and whether to trade one
afterwards.
For raids this is the whole story: a raid leader in 8.3.7 cannot assign, reserve, ban or master
loot anything. A guild distributes loot by people rolling nothing, trading what they do not need
through the trade window, and by social rules that the game does not enforce.

5. THE ROLL WINDOW: NEED, GREED, DISENCHANT, PASS
=================================================

5.1  When a roll starts
-----------------------
A roll is created for an item only if all of the following hold (Loot::FillLoot and
LootRoll::TryToStart):

  - the group's loot method is GROUP_LOOT or NEED_BEFORE_GREED (MASTER_LOOT blocks the item in
    the same way but starts no roll - the master looter assigns it);
  - the item follows loot rules (not a quest item, not free-for-all, not currency), has quality
    at or above the group's loot threshold, and is an actual item;
  - at least two players are eligible to loot it. With one eligible player no roll happens at
    all and the item is marked under-threshold for that player.
The roll does not start at the kill. It starts the first time somebody opens the corpse:
Loot::OnLootOpened walks every blocked item and starts a roll for each. All blocked items of
that corpse roll at the same time, so a boss with four drops sends four roll windows at once. If
nobody opens the corpse, no roll exists and the loot sits there.
While a roll is open the item is blocked: every looter sees it as LOOT_SLOT_TYPE_ROLL_ONGOING
(1) and cannot take it. On retail the same window is drawn as the shrinking bar with the item
icon and the dice, coin, disenchant and pass buttons.

5.2  The four buttons
---------------------

| Button     | Sends | Rule                                    |
|------------|-------|-----------------------------------------|
| Need       | 1     | 1-100; beats greed and disenchant       |
| Greed      | 2     | 1-100; wins only if nobody needs        |
| Disenchant | 3     | needs an enchanter; competes with greed |
| Pass       | 0     | no number; forfeits the item            |

Need is offered to everyone, with two exceptions: greed-only items, and - in need before greed -
players who cannot use the item (section 7). Disenchant is offered only when the item has a
disenchant loot id and some member has the Enchanting skill the item requires. Pass is always
available.
Disenchant competes with Greed, not with Need: if nobody rolls Need, the highest of the greed
and disenchant rolls takes the item (wiki). If a disenchanter wins, the item is destroyed and
the materials are stored in the winner's bags (LootRoll::Finish casts Disenchant, spell 13262).
On retail a Need roll is expected to be an upgrade for the character's main specialization;
rolling Need on everything is treated as ninja looting, but the game itself never checks whether
you need it - only whether you may press the button (section 7).

5.3  The timer
--------------
The client draws a shrinking bar for exactly the time the server sends in the StartLootRoll
packet. TrinityCore sends LOOT_ROLL_TIMEOUT = 1 minute, i.e. 60,000 ms. When it expires the roll
is resolved with whatever votes exist; players who never voted lose, and the server broadcasts
their non-vote as a pass. A vote cannot be changed once sent - PlayerVote rejects a second vote
from the same GUID.
A player who has switched on the client's auto-pass option is not sent the window at all:
TryToStart seeds that player's vote as Pass, so the roll may not even start if only one active
voter remains. On the wire the option is CMSG_OPT_OUT_OF_LOOT with PassOnLoot = 1, stored as
Player::m_bPassOnGroupLoot (default false).

5.4  Resolution
---------------

| Outcome          | What the server does                             |
|------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| Need rolled      | highest Need wins, whatever greed rolled         |
| No Need          | highest greed or disenchant wins                 |
| All passed       | SMSG_LOOT_ALL_PASSED; item becomes free loot     |
| No votes in time | same as everybody passing                        |
| Winner           | SMSG_LOOT_ROLL_WON; item stored in winner's bags |
| Bags full        | only the winner may then loot it from the corpse |

Because the winner's item is stored directly, no companion has to walk to the corpse or send a
loot request for an item it won. It only has to clean up its own roll state.

6. WHICH ITEMS ARE ROLLED ON
============================

6.1  The quality threshold
--------------------------
Every item of uncommon quality or better is rolled for by default; items below the threshold go
straight to the player who opened the corpse (wiki: "By default, uncommon and better items are
rolled for"). TrinityCore stores the threshold per group as an ItemQualities value, default
ITEM_QUALITY_UNCOMMON = 2, and the old set-method handler accepted only 2 to 6 (uncommon to
artifact).

| Id | Quality          | Rolled by default? | Id | Quality       | Rolled by default?    |
|----|------------------|--------------------|----|---------------|-----------------------|
| 0  | Poor (grey)      | no                 | 4  | Epic (purple) | yes                   |
| 1  | Normal (white)   | no                 | 5  | Legendary     | yes                   |
| 2  | Uncommon (green) | yes - threshold    | 6  | Artifact      | yes if threshold is 6 |
| 3  | Rare (blue)      | yes                | 7  | Heirloom      | n/a - not loot        |

6.2  The five conditions an item must meet
------------------------------------------

  - follow_loot_rules is set: quest items and other conditional loot are excluded, and quest
    items are handled as separate free-for-all loot that each member takes individually;
  - the item is not freeforall: free-for-all items are per-player and never roll;
  - the loot entry is an item, not a currency or a tracking quest: currency and gold never roll;
  - the item's quality is at or above the group's threshold;
  - at least two players are eligible for it after the per-item filter.
Bind-on-Equip items roll like any other item. In a Dungeon Finder group a BoE won with Need
becomes soulbound to the winner, which is why greed is the accepted vote for a BoE nobody needs
(wiki).

6.3  Item-level switches that change the buttons
------------------------------------------------

| Switch                         | Effect on the roll window         |
|--------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| ITEM_FLAG2_CAN_ONLY_ROLL_GREED | Need removed for everybody        |
| no disenchant loot id          | Disenchant not offered            |
| no qualifying enchanter        | Disenchant not offered            |
| ITEM_FLAG_HIDE_UNUSABLE_RECIPE | hidden from non-crafters; no roll |
| unique limit reached           | Need and Greed refused            |
| unusable in an LFG group       | Need refused for that player      |

The 0x100 bit value comes from TrinityCore's 11.x headers; the 8.3.7 bit position is UNVERIFIED,
the flag itself is not.

6.4  What never enters a roll
-----------------------------

  - Gold: split automatically among the group the moment the corpse is looted (CMSG_LOOT_MONEY);
  - currencies, for example the Seal of Wartorn Fate (currency 1580);
  - quest items and quest-starting items with a per-player copy;
  - items below the threshold - they belong to whoever opened the corpse;
  - anything on a corpse with only one eligible looter.
Containers use the same rule as corpses: a chest or crate always opens for any member, but a
high-quality item inside it is rolled for exactly like a boss drop (wiki).

7. WHO IS ALLOWED TO PRESS NEED
===============================

7.1  Two different regimes
--------------------------
This is the part most companion code gets wrong. The usability filter that greys out Need is not
global - it applies to Dungeon Finder groups only.

| Situation                    | Need allowed?                       |
|------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| NBG, LFG group, that dungeon | only if the player can use the item |
| NBG outside an LFG group     | everybody                           |
| GROUP_LOOT anywhere          | everybody                           |
| MASTER_LOOT                  | no rolls; master looter assigns     |

TrinityCore implements exactly this in Player::CanRollNeedForItem(proto, map, restrictOnlyLfg =
true): the function returns OK immediately when the player is not in an LFG group or the loot
object is not inside the LFG dungeon. In NEED_BEFORE_GREED the server then clears the Need bit
per player in SendStartRoll when that call fails.

7.2  The checks, in code order
------------------------------

  - class: the item's AllowableClass mask must contain the player's class;
  - race: the item's AllowableRace must contain the player's race;
  - required spell: if the item requires a spell the player does not know, refuse;
  - required skill: profession or skill rank too low, refuse;
  - weapon proficiency: an item of class weapon whose skill the player has at 0, refuse;
  - armour proficiency: an item of class armour whose subclass bit is not in the class's armour
    mask, refuse - cloaks are exempt (INVTYPE_CLOAK skips the check);
  - unique count: the player already holds the maximum number of that item, refuse.
Weapon proficiency exists as passive spells in the 8.3.7 client: 196 one-handed axes, 197
two-handed axes, 198 one-handed maces, 199 two-handed maces, 200 polearms, 201 one-handed
swords, 202 two-handed swords, 227 staves, 264 bows, 266 guns, 5011 crossbows, 1180 daggers, 674
dual wield, plus the armour spells in 7.3.

7.3  Armour proficiency in the 8.3.7 client
-------------------------------------------
The 8.3.7 ChrClasses table has no armour-mask column, so the client stores armour proficiency as
skill lines and spells. ItemSubClass ids: 1 cloth, 2 leather, 3 mail, 4 plate, 5 cosmetic, 6
shield. The matching passive spells are 9078 cloth, 9077 leather, 8737 mail, 750 plate, 9116
shield, and the skill lines are 415, 414, 413, 293 and 433 respectively. SkillRaceClassInfo
lists who holds each one:

| Armour  | Skill line | Spell | Classes holding it (build 35662)              |
|---------|------------|-------|-----------------------------------------------|
| Cloth   | 415        | 9078  | all twelve                                    |
| Leather | 414        | 9077  | War, Pal, Hun, Rog, DK, Sham, Monk, Druid, DH |
| Mail    | 413        | 8737  | Hun, Sham (min 1); War, Pal, DK (min 0)       |
| Plate   | 293        | 750   | War, Pal (min 1); DK (min 0)                  |
| Shield  | 433        | 9116  | War, Pal, Sham                                |

The extra rows - mail and leather held by Warrior, Paladin and Death Knight at minimum level 0 -
are lower-tier entries the client still carries. A server must therefore read the highest armour
type a class learns, not simply "does the skill line exist". Whether the 8.3.7 client itself
uses these rows, the ChrClasses mask, or the item's AllowableClass to grey out Need is
UNVERIFIED - and it never runs on retail, because retail never opens a roll window.

7.4  Why a companion should not re-implement this
-------------------------------------------------
The server already did the work. SMSG_START_LOOT_ROLL carries a per-player ValidRolls bitmask
(0x01 pass, 0x02 need, 0x04 greed, 0x08 disenchant), and TrinityCore clears bits per player -
Need for an unusable item, Disenchant when no enchanter qualifies. A companion that only ever
sends a vote whose bit is set can never be refused. Section 12 builds the rule on that.

8. ROLL NUMBERS, TIMING, TIES
=============================

| Question      | Answer                  | Source                               |
|---------------|-------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Roll range    | 1-100 inclusive         | urand(1, 100) in PlayerVote          |
| Window        | 60,000 ms               | LOOT_ROLL_TIMEOUT = 1min             |
| Vote change   | not possible            | PlayerVote rejects a second vote     |
| Numbers live? | no in TrinityCore       | Need sent as 0, others as -1         |
| Tie           | hidden re-roll (retail) | wiki; TC keeps the first, no re-roll |
| Non-voter     | counted as pass         | SendLootRollWon sends Pass           |

8.1  Priority and arithmetic
----------------------------
Need beats greed and disenchant absolutely. A single Need roll of 1 beats a Greed roll of 100.
Among Need rolls the highest number wins; if nobody rolled Need, greed and disenchant numbers
compete in one pool. This is true on retail and in TrinityCore.
The number itself is generated server-side; the client only displays it. Nothing a companion
sends can influence it - there is no client-supplied roll value in CMSG_LOOT_ROLL.

8.2  Ties
---------
The wiki says the retail client performs a hidden secondary roll when two players tie, and the
loser is simply resolved by that extra roll. TrinityCore does not re-roll: AllPlayerVoted keeps
the first entry whose number is strictly greater, so with equal numbers the winner is whichever
GUID the vote map iterates first. Treat the tie-break as unpredictable and never write a
companion that assumes it will win a tie.

8.3  What a companion sees and when
-----------------------------------

  - at roll start: one SMSG_START_LOOT_ROLL per blocked item, all at once, each with its own
    LootListID and the full RollTime;
  - as votes arrive: SMSG_LOOT_ROLL per voter, carrying the voter's GUID and RollType;
  - at the end: SMSG_LOOT_ROLL_WON (if somebody won) or SMSG_LOOT_ALL_PASSED (if nobody did),
    then the final SMSG_LOOT_ROLL with the real numbers.

9. TRADING LOOT INSIDE THE GROUP (THE TWO-HOUR WINDOW)
======================================================

9.1  The retail rule
--------------------
A Bind-on-Pickup item looted in an instance can be traded to other members of the group for up
to two hours after the drop (wiki, Bind on Pickup: "in the case of instance drops the items may
be traded for up to two hours after they dropped"). Two further conditions apply:

  - the item must not be an item-level upgrade for the player who received it - the wiki's
    personal-loot paragraph says loot "is tradeable to anyone in the group, as long as it is
    also not an ilvl upgrade for yourself";
  - the receiver must have been eligible for that loot - a player who joined after the kill, or
    who was not in the group, cannot be given the item.
Enchanting, socketing or otherwise modifying the item makes it untradeable immediately (wiki).
Trading is one-way in practice: once received, the item is soulbound to the new owner and cannot
be passed on again.

9.2  Who was eligible
---------------------
Eligibility is fixed at the moment the loot is created, not at the moment you trade. TrinityCore
records it as the item's allowed-looter set: every group member within group-reward distance of
the corpse when the loot was generated. Inside a dungeon or raid that distance test always
succeeds - Player::IsAtGroupRewardDistance returns true for any distance when the map is an
instance - so in an instance the set is the whole group. Outdoors it is the MaxGroupXPDistance
radius, default 74 yards (worldserver.conf.dist).
The client is told whether an item is tradeable through the CanTradeToTapList bit in the loot
packet, which TrinityCore sets to allowedGUIDs.size() > 1 - the item is tradeable exactly when
more than one player could have looted it.

9.3  How the trade itself works
-------------------------------

  - either player opens a trade on the other's portrait or by dragging an item onto them
    (CMSG_BEGIN_TRADE);
  - items go into the trade slots, money into the money fields; changing anything drops the
    other side's acceptance (CMSG_SET_TRADE_ITEM);
  - both press Trade (CMSG_ACCEPT_TRADE); if either moves out of trade range the trade cancels;
  - trading works in combat and cannot be started with a character who is logging out.

9.4  What TrinityCore actually implements
-----------------------------------------
The retail rule has three parts and TrinityCore implements roughly one and a half of them:

| Part of the rule        | Retail 8.3.7      | TrinityCore                               |
|-------------------------|-------------------|-------------------------------------------|
| Item can be traded      | if not an upgrade | only if the server flags it - normal loot |
|                         |                   | awards are not flagged (empty looter set) |
| Trade window length     | 2 hours real time | 4 hours of /played time                   |
| Only eligible looters   | yes               | yes - the allowed-GUID set                |
| Item-level upgrade test | yes               | not implemented                           |
| One trade only          | yes               | flag is not cleared by the trade itself   |

The timer is Item::CheckSoulboundTradeExpire: an item stops being tradeable when
CreatePlayedTime + 4 hours is less than the owner's total played time, i.e. four hours of
in-game time, which is about two hours of continuous play. Item::SetSoulboundTradeable is the
only way an item becomes tradeable, and in the stock code it is reached from
Player::StoreNewItem only when more than one allowed looter is passed in, and from the
character-load restore of the item_soulbound_trade_data table.
Loot::StoreLootItem calls StoreNewItem with an empty set, so personal-loot and roll-won items
are bound on receipt. LootHandler::HandleLootMasterGiveOpcode passes item.GetAllowedLooters(),
so a master-looted item is tradeable to the group. If you want retail behaviour on a 8.3.7
server you must add the item-level comparison and pass the looter set in the normal loot path.

10.2  The answer for a TrinityCore server
-----------------------------------------

  - there is no per-difficulty loot switch - the method is a property of the group, not of the
    instance;
  - a new group is PERSONAL_LOOT with threshold UNCOMMON, and both values are persisted in
    characters.group (lootMethod, lootThreshold);
  - the client cannot change either value on current code; an old handler would allow
    FREE_FOR_ALL, MASTER_LOOT, GROUP_LOOT or PERSONAL_LOOT, never inside an LFG group;
  - therefore: to make your companions answer a roll window you must first set GROUP_LOOT or
    NEED_BEFORE_GREED on the group, by SQL or by a script.

12. COMPANION BEHAVIOUR: ANSWERING A ROLL WINDOW
================================================

This section is the action specification for a computer-controlled character. It assumes the
group is on GROUP_LOOT or NEED_BEFORE_GREED, because that is the only situation in which a roll
window exists.

12.1  What arrives, and what it carries
---------------------------------------

| Packet               | Dir  | Fields the companion must read                                |
|----------------------|------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| SMSG_START_LOOT_ROLL | S->C | LootObj, MapID, Item.LootListID, item id, quantity,           |
|                      |      | item context, RollTime (ms), ValidRolls mask, Method          |
| SMSG_LOOT_ROLL       | S->C | LootObj, voter GUID, roll number, RollType                    |
| SMSG_LOOT_ROLL_WON   | S->C | winner GUID, roll number, RollType                            |
| SMSG_LOOT_ALL_PASSED | S->C | LootObj, LootListID - the item is now free on the corpse      |
| CMSG_LOOT_ROLL       | C->S | LootObj, LootListID, RollType (0 pass, 1 need, 2 greed, 3 DE) |

The RollType values are fixed: 0 pass, 1 need, 2 greed, 3 disenchant. 4 exists in the enum as
transmogrify but is not part of the 0x0F valid mask in this build, so never send it.

12.2  The answering procedure
-----------------------------

  1. On SMSG_START_LOOT_ROLL, read the pair (LootObj, LootListID). If that pair is already in
     the answered set, ignore the packet - the server rejects a second vote anyway.
  2. Read ValidRolls. This is the per-player truth: the server already removed Need from a
     player who cannot use the item, and Disenchant when no enchanter qualifies.
  3. Decide intent from the item, not from the mask: Need if it is an item-level upgrade for the
     companion's loot specialization, Greed if it is wanted for off-spec, transmog or sale,
     Disenchant if the companion is the group enchanter and the materials beat the item, Pass
     otherwise.
  4. Clamp intent to the mask: if intent is Need and 0x02 is not set, downgrade to Greed; if
     Greed is not set either, use Disenchant if 0x08 is set, else Pass.
  5. Send CMSG_LOOT_ROLL with that RollType before RollTime runs out. Leave a margin - a vote
     that arrives after the window is simply not counted.
  6. Add the pair to the answered set. Do not send a second packet for the same pair, and do not
     send one for a LootListID you never received a window for.
  7. On SMSG_LOOT_ROLL_WON with your GUID: nothing to do. The item is already stored in your
     bags by the server; do not walk to the corpse and do not send CMSG_LOOT_ITEM for it.
  8. On SMSG_LOOT_ALL_PASSED for a pair: the item is now free loot (LOOT_SLOT_TYPE_ALLOW_LOOT
     for everyone). To take it, open the corpse with CMSG_LOOT and send CMSG_LOOT_ITEM with that
     LootListID.
  9. On SMSG_LOOT_ROLL_WON with somebody else's GUID: clear the pair and move on. The item is
     gone.
  10. If the companion never answers - disconnect, stun, script error - it is counted as a pass
     at expiry. There is no way to recover the vote.

12.3  Decision table
--------------------

| Item evaluation                  | Best vote                      | If the bit is missing    |
|----------------------------------|--------------------------------|--------------------------|
| upgrade for the active loot spec | Need (1)                       | Greed (2)                |
| upgrade for the other spec       | Need (1) if no main-spec rival | Greed (2)                |
| transmog, mount, toy, recipe     | Greed (2)                      | Disenchant (3) if 0x08   |
| enchanter, item disenchantable   | Disenchant (3)                 | Greed (2)                |
| not wanted, not usable           | Pass (0)                       | -                        |
| below the group threshold        | no window arrives              | the looter keeps it      |
| personal loot group              | no window arrives              | loot your own slots only |

12.4  Personal loot: what the companion does instead
----------------------------------------------------

  - open the corpse with CMSG_LOOT; the response lists only the companion's own slots;
  - every slot whose UIType is 4 (LOOT_SLOT_TYPE_OWNER) is the companion's - take each with
    CMSG_LOOT_ITEM, bags permitting;
  - take the money with CMSG_LOOT_MONEY, which splits it across the group;
  - close with CMSG_LOOT_RELEASE so the corpse does not stay busy.

12.5  Edge cases that break naive implementations
-------------------------------------------------

| Case                     | Correct behaviour                            |
|--------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| four items roll at once  | answer each (LootObj, LootListID)            |
| nobody opens the corpse  | no roll starts; open it yourself             |
| bags full, you win       | you alone may loot it from the corpse        |
| auto-pass is on          | no windows; send CMSG_OPT_OUT_OF_LOOT with 0 |
| greed-only item          | Need bit clear; greed or DE only             |
| dead in an instance      | still eligible; window still arrives         |
| loading when roll starts | window missed; the vote is a pass            |
| tied numbers             | unpredictable in TC; never assume a win      |
| master loot group        | no windows; the master assigns               |
| legacy loot instance     | no windows; loot the corpse                  |

13.6  What has no id
--------------------

  - creature entries: no NPC, gameobject or scripted creature takes part in loot distribution.
    Loot is generated per creature corpse from that creature's loot template, so the same rules
    apply to every entry in the game;
  - coordinates: no location is part of the rule. The only two distances that matter are the
    reward radius (ignored inside instances, 74 yd outdoors) and trade range (both players must
    stay inside it or the trade cancels);
  - item ids: the roll window carries whatever item dropped, so there is no fixed item to quote.
    The only fixed item-adjacent ids are the two currencies in 13.2.

==============

Items that could not be verified. Each one is either left out of the rules above or used with an
explicit warning:

  - the exact personal-loot item coefficient in 8.3.7, including the solo-raid 20% figure. The
    20%-per-player ratio is community reproduction, not a Blizzard statement; section 2.2 uses
    it as a shape, not a rule.
  - whether a bonus-rolled item (Seal of Wartorn Fate) can be traded in 8.3.7.
  - the 8.3.7 bit position of ITEM_FLAG2_CAN_ONLY_ROLL_GREED; the 0x100 value is from
    TrinityCore's 11.x headers.
  - how the 8.3.7 client itself decides to grey out Need: SkillRaceClassInfo rows, an armour
    mask, or the item's AllowableClass. The rows in 7.3 are client data; the client's use of
    them is not observable on retail, because retail never opens a roll window.
  - the retail tie-break. The wiki describes a hidden secondary roll; TrinityCore does not
    re-roll, so the two conflict.
  - whether roll numbers are drawn live on a retail 8.3.7 client. TrinityCore hides them until
    the roll resolves.
  - the numeric opcode ids for build 35662.
  - whether the 8.3.7 client still renders a roll frame at all. The packets and the vote path
    exist in the 8.x-derived code, but no retail 8.3.7 server sends them.
  - that a traded Bind-on-Pickup item cannot be traded a second time. This is well-known game
    behaviour but the 2020 wiki does not state it.
  - trade distance and the number of trade slots in 8.3.7.
  - the loot method used by island expeditions, warfronts and Horrific Visions individually.
    Section 10 marks them personal because personal loot is the only method in the build, not
    because each one was documented.
  - the defaults of a specific TrinityCore 8.3.7 fork. No official 8.3.7 branch exists; the code
    statements quoted here are from the current master branch.
