1. HOW TO READ THIS DOCUMENT
============================

Coordinates are written (map, x, y, z) in the client's own yards and are the exact spawn points
used by the server, not map-pin estimates, unless the row says POI. 'POI' means the coordinate
is the AreaPOI row the client itself draws on the battleground map; where both exist they differ
by a few yards because the POI sits on the icon and the server point sits on the flag or banner.
A route is given as an ordered list of anchor points. It is not a navmesh. The static data
available to a companion author - DBC/DB2, the TrinityCore scripts and the wiki - contains no
terrain walkability information, so a route cannot be proven 'unstuck' from this data alone.
What is given is every point a character must reach, in the order that avoids doubling back,
with the chokepoints named. The character itself must path between anchors with the server's own
pathfinder (TrinityCore's MovePoint/MovePath use the mmaps tiles); a straight line between two
anchors is not guaranteed to be walkable and is not claimed to be.
Three kinds of number appear in the tables and they are not interchangeable:
  - Client data - Map.db2, BattlemasterList.db2, AreaPOI.db2, PvpStat.db2, ItemSparse.db2,
    Item.db2, Heirloom.db2, SpellItemEnchantment.db2, ItemEffect.db2, SpellEffect.db2 and
    SpellName.db2, all read from build 35662.
  - Server data - TrinityCore tag 8.3.7/35662 (the 8.3.7 release tag still exists upstream). Its
    Battlegrounds/Zones files carry the spawn points, the creature and gameobject entries and
    the reward constants used by a private 8.3.7 core. Where the tag and the live game disagree,
    both numbers are printed and the difference is explained.
  - Live-game rules - 2020 revisions of the Warcraft Wiki articles, which describe the retail
    behaviour the client expects. These are the numbers a player sees; a TrinityCore realm only
    sees them if its own constants match.
Battlegrounds that TrinityCore never implemented in 8.3.7 - Silvershard Mines, Temple of
Kotmogu, Deepwind Gorge, Seething Shore and Ashran - are documented from client POI data and
wiki rules, and the TrinityCore 12.x scripts are used only where the geometry is unchanged.
Those rows are marked as such.

3. ROUTES: START TO OBJECTIVES, FACTION BY FACTION
==================================================

Each subsection gives the anchors in walking order, then the mount or vehicle rule. Anchors are
spawn points of flags, banners, spirit guides and bosses, so a character that reaches an anchor
is standing exactly where the server expects the interaction to happen. Where a battleground has
no verifiable coordinates the subsection says so and gives the landmark route instead: that
happens for two battlegrounds in full and two in part, and section 8 names them with the reason.


3.1 Warsong Gulch (maps 489 and 2106)
-------------------------------------
Two bases facing each other across a valley with a river and a tunnel network. Alliance holds
Silverwing Hold in the north, Horde holds Warsong Lumber Mill in the south. The two flag rooms
are inside the keeps; the two graveyards sit outside and below each keep, so a resurrected
character must walk up to the base rather than spawn on the flag.

   Verified ids: graveyard 769 (Alliance flag room), 770 (Horde flag
   room), 771 (Alliance main), 772 (Horde main); exploit teleport
   locations 3784 and 3785. No AreaPOI rows exist on 489 or 2106, and
   the 8.3.7 scripts spawn the flags as world-DB objects, so no x/y/z
   can be quoted from these sources: UNVERIFIED.

Landmark route, Alliance carrier: Silverwing Hold flag room -> north ramp -> tunnel mouth ->
river crossing at the centre -> south ramp -> Warsong Lumber Mill flag room. Return on the same
line. The tunnel is the only covered route; the ramp is the choke a defender camps.
Mount: riding is allowed outdoors, and picking the flag up dismounts and breaks stealth. A
carrier cannot mount, so the escort mounts and the carrier walks. No vehicle exists on this map.

3.7 Twin Peaks (map 726)
------------------------
Capture the flag, 10v10, same rules as Warsong Gulch. Alliance holds the Wildhammer Stronghold
on the northern plateau, Horde holds the Dragonmaw compound in the south over the water. A
single bridge crosses the river in the centre; the river itself is crossable.
No AreaPOI rows exist on map 726 and the 8.3.7 scripts spawn the flags from the world database,
so no x/y/z can be quoted from client or core data: UNVERIFIED. The route is: base flag room ->
plateau ramp (Alliance) or compound bridge (Horde) -> centre bridge -> enemy base -> flag room.
Each faction has two graveyards, which is why a kill near the enemy base does not always send
the character back to the start.
Mount: allowed; the plateau ramps are the only climb. No vehicle.

4. ALTERAC VALLEY IN FULL
=========================

Map 30, BattlemasterList 1, 40 v 40, minimum level 20 in the 8.3.7 client. Two fortified bases
at opposite ends of a valley, four towers per side, seven capturable graveyards plus two
uncapturable base graveyards, two mines, two captains, two generals, and three wing commanders
per faction.

4.1 Win condition and the reinforcement pool
--------------------------------------------
A team loses when its reinforcements reach 0. Killing the enemy general sets the enemy pool to 0
immediately, so the general is both the win condition and the fastest route to it. Everything
else in the valley is a way of draining the pool faster than the other team.
The pool size is the one number on this page where the sources do not agree with each other:
| Source                      | Reinforcements | Note                                       |
|-----------------------------|----------------|--------------------------------------------|
| TrinityCore tag 8.3.7/35662 |            600 | BG_AV_SCORE_INITIAL_POINTS                 |
| Wiki article body           |            700 | 'Each team starts with 700'                |
| Wiki patch history          |            600 | 600 to 500 (2012-09-18), +100 (2018-07-27) |
The patch arithmetic is 600 minus 100 in 2012, then plus 100 in 2018, which returns to 600 - the
value the 8.3.7 core constant uses. The article body's 700 is not supported by the article's own
patch list and is not reproducible from it, so 600 is the number to build against. The 2018
hotfix also stated that NPC health and damage now scale with player level and gear, which is why
AV NPCs in 8.3.7 are not the fixed-value mobs of older expansions.

4.3 How a node is taken
-----------------------
Click the flag, stay alive for the capture timer, and the node flips. The timers are constants
in the 8.3.7 header:
| Action                   |                        Time | Constant                       |
|--------------------------|-----------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Graveyard capture        |                       4 min | BG_AV_CAPTIME 240000           |
| Snowfall first capture   | 5 min (core) / 4 min (live) | BG_AV_SNOWFALL_FIRSTCAP 300000 |
| Tower burn               |                       4 min | BG_AV_CAPTIME                  |
| Mine reclaim (ownerless) |                      20 min | AV_MINE_RECLAIM_TIMER          |
A tower does not become 'captured': it becomes contested, then burns. Burning is permanent and
kills the marshal or warmaster linked to it, weakening the general. A graveyard goes contested,
then owned; the enemy can flip it back by clicking while it is contested, which is why every
graveyard fight is a two-click race.
Snowfall is the exception. It starts neutral, and its first capture is longer than an ordinary
one. The 8.3.7 core constant is 300000 (five minutes) and the header even comments '5:00 but i
also have seen 4:05'; the live game shortened it to four minutes in patch 2.4.0 ('reduced from
five minutes to four'). A realm matches retail only if it changes the constant.

| Guard                      | Entry | Dies when this burns    |
|----------------------------|-------|-------------------------|
| Marshal (Dun Baldar South) | 14763 | Dun Baldar South Bunker |

On live, each surviving guard increases the health and damage of the general and of the other
guards by a stacking 25%, so with all four up the general sits at +144% of both - the wiki's own
figure - which is why no group kills a general before the towers. The 8.3.7 core does not
implement that stack: it spawns the four guards and deletes one when its tower falls, nothing
more. A companion should assume the live behaviour is absent on a TrinityCore realm and treat
the guards as four ordinary elites.
Captains are separate. While a captain lives, it periodically casts a flat 20% health buff on
everyone of its faction: 23693 Stormpike's Salvation for the Alliance, 22751 Fury of the
Frostwolf for the Horde. The core recasts it every 2 to 6 minutes and the buff itself lasts 2
minutes. Killing the enemy captain drains 100 reinforcements and removes the buff for the rest
of the match.
There are no vehicles in Alterac Valley. Every character moves on foot or on a personal mount,
and the only map objects that move are the two mine garrisons and the wing commanders.

4.5 The two mines
-----------------
Mines are captured by killing the mine's boss, not by clicking. The owner regains one
reinforcement every 45 seconds (AV_MINE_TICK_TIMER 45000) for as long as it holds, which is
small against a 600 pool but decisive in a long attrition match.

| Coldtooth Mine (south) | -946.91 | -195.45 | 76.96 | kobolds                    |
Coordinates are the AreaPOI pins; the mines have no flag object. A mine that is neither team's
is reclaimed after 20 minutes and its NPCs respawn neutral.

4.6 Wing commanders
-------------------
Three per faction, held in enemy territory. Freeing one sends it running home; when it arrives
it awards bonus honor and a turn-in quest. Worth knowing before a companion chases the column:
the client's 'Secondary Objectives' column is fed by mines captured in the 8.3.7 core, not by
wing commanders, and the wing-commander score constant (BG_AV_GET_COMMANDER) is defined but
never applied.

|----------|------------------------------------------|-----------------------|
| Alliance | Wing Commander Slidore / Vipore / Ichman | in Horde territory    |
| Horde    | Wing Commander Guse / Jeztor / Mulverick | in Alliance territory |

4.7 What each event does to the reinforcements
----------------------------------------------
| Event                              | Effect                     | Once per                |
|------------------------------------|----------------------------|-------------------------|
| Player killed                      | -1 to killed player's team | always                  |
| Captain killed                     | -100                       | once per match          |
| Tower or bunker burned             | -75                        | 8 per match, 4 per side |
| All four towers of one side burned | -300 total                 | sum of the above        |
| Mine held                          | +1 per 45 s                | per mine, per tick      |
| General killed                     | enemy pool set to 0        | match ends              |
The arithmetic a companion needs: four towers and the captain are 400 of a 600 pool, so a clean
tower-and-captain game wins on reinforcements without ever touching the general. If the enemy
team is trading kills at 1 each, a match that reaches 600 kills is already over.

4.8 Honor values
----------------
The 8.3.7 header stores these as kill-honor multipliers, and the server converts them to honor
at the player's level; the reputation values are flat.
| Event                           | Honor x | Reputation |
|---------------------------------|---------|------------|
| General killed                  |       4 |        350 |
| Captain killed                  |       3 |        125 |
| Tower or bunker burned          |       3 |         12 |
| Own tower standing at match end |       2 |         12 |
| Own captain alive at match end  |       2 |        125 |
| Wing commander returned         |       1 |         -- |
Constants, in order: BG_AV_KILL_BOSS / BG_AV_REP_BOSS, BG_AV_KILL_CAPTAIN / BG_AV_REP_CAPTAIN,
BG_AV_KILL_TOWER / BG_AV_REP_TOWER, BG_AV_KILL_SURVIVING_TOWER, BG_AV_KILL_SURVIVING_CAPTAIN,
BG_AV_GET_COMMANDER. The multiplier is applied to the kill-honor value for the player's level;
the reputation number is flat.

4.9 The order a good group does it
----------------------------------
  1. Two defenders leave the group at Stormpike Graveyard and hold the Dun Baldar bridge;
     everyone else mounts and rides south as one body.
  2. Kill Galvangar at Iceblood Garrison (entry 11947 at -545.23 / -165.35 / 57.79): 100
     reinforcements and the Horde loses its 20% health buff.
  3. Split: one group burns Iceblood Tower, another takes Iceblood Graveyard. The tower is
     permanent, the graveyard is a respawn point.
  4. Burn Tower Point, then push to Frostwolf Graveyard and cap it. At this point two of the
     four Horde towers are gone and two warmasters with them.
  5. Send a stealth group ahead to burn East and West Frostwolf Tower while the main group caps
     Frostwolf Graveyard.
  6. Cap the Relief Hut. With all four towers burning, Drek'Thar has no warmasters left.
  7. Pull Drek'Thar with the whole group inside the room; the General is a raid boss and resets
     if pulled out.
  8. If the enemy is ahead on towers, do not chase the general: trade graveyards, hold both
     mines and win on the pool.
The Horde order is the exact mirror, with Balinda, Stonehearth, Icewing, the two Dun Baldar
bunkers, the Aid Station and Vanndar.


5. ISLE OF CONQUEST IN FULL
===========================

Map 628, BattlemasterList 30, 40 v 40, minimum level 20 in the 8.3.7 client. Two keeps facing
each other across an island, five capturable points, three kinds of siege vehicle, an airship,
and a reinforcement pool that ends the match if nobody reaches the enemy general.

5.1 Win condition and the reinforcement pool
--------------------------------------------
A team loses at 0 reinforcements, and every player death costs that team one reinforcement.
Killing the enemy general ends the match immediately. Both cores agree on the pool size, which
is not true of Alterac Valley:
| Source                      | Reinforcements | Where                     |
|-----------------------------|----------------|---------------------------|
| TrinityCore tag 8.3.7/35662 |            300 | MAX_REINFORCEMENTS 300    |
| 2020 wiki                   |            300 | each team starts with 300 |
The TrinityCore 12.x branch raised this to 400; the 8.3.7 tag and the wiki both say 300, so 300
is the 8.3.7 value.

5.2 The five capture points
---------------------------
All five are taken the same way: click the banner and the point goes contested for 60 seconds
(BANNER_STATE_CHANGE_TIME 60000), after which the real faction banner spawns and the point is
yours. Nothing has to be channelled and the clicker does not have to stay; standing on a
contested point does not speed it up. The counter-play is that an enemy who clicks the same
banner during those 60 seconds starts the timer again in their own favour, which is why
contested points change hands in pairs and why a companion defending a point must click the
banner the moment it turns grey.

|----------|----------------|---------|----------|--------|----------------------------|
| Docks    |         195157 |  726.39 |  -360.21 |  17.82 | glaive throwers, catapults |
| Hangar   |         195158 |  807.78 | -1000.07 | 132.38 | airship, parachutes        |
| Workshop |         195133 |  776.23 |  -804.28 |   6.45 | demolishers, siege engine  |
| Quarry   |         195338 |  251.02 | -1159.32 |  17.24 | +15% siege damage          |
| Refinery |         195343 | 1269.50 |  -400.81 |  37.63 | +15% siege damage          |
The two keep graveyards are also capturable points in the same sense: Horde keep banner 195393
at 1284.76 / -705.67 / 48.92, Alliance keep banner 195396 at 299.15 / -784.59 / 48.92. Neither
can be taken until one of that keep's three gates is destroyed.

5.6 Refinery and Quarry
-----------------------
Holding the Refinery or the Quarry gives the owning team +15% damage from all siege vehicles,
applied to each player as a spell on login and on resurrect (68720 Quarry, 68719 Refinery). The
Refinery, the Quarry and the Docks also pay one reinforcement and 12 honor per 45-second tick.
That trickle is the reason a losing team can stall: at 300 reinforcements, one point returning a
reinforcement every 45 seconds (IC_RESOURCE_TIME 45000) offsets exactly one player death per 45
seconds, and all three points together offset three. The 8.3.7 source comments the interval as
'not sure, needs more research', so treat 45 seconds as the server's value rather than a
confirmed retail one.

5.7 The airship
---------------
Holding the Hangar docks an airship with five gunship cannons and grants everyone on board a
parachute. The three boarding portals are at 827.92/-993.32/134.20, 739.02/-1106.66/134.76 and
672.08/-1156.78/133.71. A character that jumps from the airship at the enemy keep lands inside
the walls without breaking a gate, which makes the Hangar the fastest route to the enemy general
and the hardest point to hold.

5.8 What each event does
------------------------
| Event                   | Effect                        | Once per           |
|-------------------------|-------------------------------|--------------------|
| Player killed           | -1 to killed player's team    | always             |
| Point captured          | node ownership, vehicles      | 5 points + 2 keeps |
| Refinery or Quarry held | +15% siege dmg, +1 reinf/45 s | per point          |
| Gate destroyed          | courtyard opens, keep GY      | 1 of 3 per keep    |
| General killed          | enemy pool set to 0           | match ends         |

5.9 The order a good group does it
----------------------------------
  1. Everyone rides to the Workshop first: it is equidistant, and it is the only point that
     gives both demolishers and a siege engine.
  2. Four people take the Workshop while the rest screen the enemy; the siege engine assembles
     itself three minutes after the capture.
  3. Send a second group to the Docks for glaive throwers, or to the Hangar if the enemy already
     has the Docks. Never split for both.
  4. Take the Refinery or the Quarry for the +15% siege damage; the two are worth the same and
     only one is needed.
  5. Drive the siege engine at a gate while demolishers hit the same gate. Two vehicles on one
     gate is faster than four on three gates.
  6. Once a gate falls, take the keep graveyard and push the general's room. Keep cannons kill
     vehicles, so infantry goes up the walls first.
  7. If the enemy is ahead on the pool, stop attacking and hold all five points: at 300
     reinforcements attrition wins faster than a failed general pull.

5.10 What a companion should do differently from a player
---------------------------------------------------------
  - Never fight on the road between two owned points; the scoreboard counts the banner click,
    and a point nobody clicks is a point the enemy will.
  - A companion assigned to a vehicle should mount it only when a driver seat is free; the siege
    engine in particular is wasted on a passenger.
  - Parachute landings are one-way. A companion that drops into the keep cannot walk out through
    a gate it did not break.


6. THE SCOREBOARD: COLUMNS AND HOW EACH NUMBER IS COUNTED
=========================================================

The client builds the battleground scoreboard from two layers. The first is a fixed set of six
columns that every battleground has; the second is a per-map list of extra columns stored in
PvpStat.db2, which in build 35662 carries the stat name, the stat id and the map id. That table
is the authoritative answer to 'which columns does the client show', because it is the table the
client itself reads.

6.1 The six columns every battleground has
------------------------------------------
| Column          | ScoreType | Counted as                                           |
|-----------------|-----------|------------------------------------------------------|
| Killing Blows   |         1 | Kills where this player dealt the killing blow.      |
| Deaths          |         2 | Times this player died.                              |
| Honorable Kills |         3 | Enemy players killed while this player was in range. |
| Bonus Honor     |         4 | Honor awarded by objectives, not by kills.           |
| Damage Done     |         5 | Damage this player dealt to players.                 |
| Healing Done    |         6 | Healing this player did to players.                  |
The numbers are the ScoreType values in BattlegroundScore.h, which TrinityCore assigns to these
six columns in this order; the client prints them as its fixed leading columns and PvpStat.db2
adds only the per-map extras in 6.2. Damage and healing accumulate per player for the whole
match and are not reset by death.

7. LEVEL 19 TWINKS
==================

This section is a reference for a level-19 character in build 35662 and for a companion that has
to equip and maintain one. Every item id, item level, required level, enchant id and spell id
below is read from the 8.3.7 client tables; the prose says which table, so any line can be
checked. Where a number depends on level scaling that the client tables do not carry, the line
says so instead of guessing.

7.1 How a character stays at 19
-------------------------------
Experience is switched off by an NPC, not by a client setting. The two NPCs are Behsten in
Stormwind for the Alliance and Slahtz in Orgrimmar for the Horde; the toggle is a gossip option,
not a quest. The Warcraft Wiki Twink article records the mechanic being added in patch 3.2.0,
where disabling experience cost 10 gold and battlegrounds were changed so that experience-off
characters only meet other experience-off characters. Patch 4.0.1 extended that pool to
expansion-capped players.
Two consequences matter on a private realm. First, the creature entries for Behsten and Slahtz
in build 35662 are not in the creature table available here, so their ids are UNVERIFIED; a
TrinityCore realm has them from its own world database, not from the client. Second, since patch
5.2 the battleground scales a character's effective level up to the top of its bracket and
normalises health, attributes and heirlooms to that level, so a level 19 in the 10-19 bracket
fights as a levelled 19 with 19-scaled stats. A twink's edge is gear, enchants and spell ranks,
not the level gap.
The practical rule at the table: a level 19 twink is a parked character. Its spellbook, talents
and gear are frozen at 19, and a companion that follows a levelling group and dings 20 has left
the bracket.

7.2 The three gates every item and every enchant has to pass
------------------------------------------------------------
Two different questions get confused in most twink lists, so this document keeps them apart.
Gate one, the character level of the scroll. ItemSparse.RequiredLevel on the scroll item itself.
Every Enchant scroll in the 8.3.7 item table has RequiredLevel 0, so a level 19 can always use
the scroll.
Gate two, the character level range of the enchant. SpellItemEnchantment.MinLevel and MaxLevel.
TrinityCore reads MinLevel as the character level the enchant requires
(Item::GetEnchantRequiredLevel), and the columns are printed per enchant below. No legal enchant
has MinLevel above 0. The MaxLevel column runs up in expansion steps - 60 for classic, then 70,
80, 85, 90, 100, 110 and 120 - and TrinityCore does not test it at all, so it cannot be what
stops a 19: every one of those caps is above 19. Across the 473 scrolls in the table not one has
a MinLevel between 1 and 19 and not one has a MaxLevel below 19, so gate two never blocks an
enchant that gate three would allow. The nearest miss is Super Stats (38989): it needs character
level 50 and item level 55, so both gates refuse it.
Gate three, the item level of the target. SpellItemEnchantment.ItemLevel. This is the item-level
figure the client keeps for the enchant and it is the reason a level-19 twink cannot use the
whole enchant list. The figures fall into tiers: 1 for classic and Burning Crusade scrolls, 30
for the Wrath tier, 55 for the Naxxramas and Ulduar tier, 162 for the hard-mode Ulduar tier and
290 for the Icecrown tier. The gear in 7.4 to 7.8 sits at item level 21 to 24 (the two cloth and
leather helms at 10 excepted), and the only figures in the whole enchant table between 2 and 29
are 5, 10, 15, 20, 24 and 25 - the temporary enhancement families (scopes and armour kits), not
scrolls - so the scroll set a 19 can use is exactly the tier-1 group. The tier-30 Wrath scrolls
need an item at item level 30, which in this bracket means only the req-0 quest and scaling
pieces discussed in 7.13.
So the working rule for this bracket is short: a scroll is usable at 19 when its ItemLevel
figure is 1. Every enchant table in 7.9 is filtered on that rule, with the figure printed so the
reader can widen or narrow it.
Two things are not in these tables and are not guessed at anywhere below: how large a scaling
enchant's effect becomes on a level-19 item (the client stores the top-of-band figure, the
scaled-down figure is computed at run time), and where any item comes from (drops and vendors
live in the server's world database).

7.3 Bind-on-account gear that works at 19
-----------------------------------------
Heirlooms are the cheapest way to a full set and they are the only items in the bracket that
carry a scaling item level. Heirloom.db2 in build 35662 has 132 rows; 131 are wearable and 98 of
those have a required level of 19 or lower, and those 98 are listed here with the 8.3.7 item id
and the pre-8.0 item id that most guides still quote. Item level is 1 on the base row because
the real item level is computed from the wearer's level.
Armour proficiency decides who can wear what at 19, and since 7.0.3 the armour type is a class
property with no level gate at all: warrior and paladin wear plate from level 1, hunter and
shaman wear mail from level 1, rogue, druid and monk wear leather, priest, mage and warlock wear
cloth. The client proves it three ways. SkillRaceClassInfo row 293 (skill line "Plate Mail") has
ClassMask 3 - warrior and paladin - at MinLevel 1, and SkillLineAbility points it at spell 750,
which has no SpellLevels row. SkillRaceClassInfo row 413 (skill line "Mail") has ClassMask 68 -
hunter and shaman - at MinLevel 1, and its ability spell 8737 (Mail) has BaseLevel 1 in
SpellLevels, so there is no level-40 gate anywhere in the 8.3.7 data. Rows 414 (Leather,
ClassMask 3695) and 415 (Cloth, ClassMask all) sit at level 0, and row 433 is the shield skill.
So the plate heirlooms (Polished Valor, and every other plate set) are wearable at 19 by a
warrior or paladin, the mail heirlooms (Champion's Deathdealer 122379, Mystical Vest of Elements
122380, Champion Herod's Shoulder 122356) are wearable at 19 by a warrior, paladin, hunter or
shaman, and the "mail until 40" rule that most 2010-era twink guides print is pre-7.0.3 and does
not describe this build.

Heirlooms take any enchant that does not bind the item. The Warcraft Wiki Heirloom article
(revision 1585052, 2020-08-05) states it plainly: "Heirlooms can be enchanted only with
enhancements that will not bind the item to the character. All enchantments can be applied to

their power scaled down to be similar to enhancements intended for that level range. Note that
profession specific enchantments still require the profession." That sentence is the licence for
every enchant in 7.9: the heirloom does not refuse the scroll, and the effect is scaled down to
the 19 band.
Shoulder inscriptions are the one class of enhancement a 19 cannot apply itself. The four Sons
of Hodir items are in the heirloom table: Greater Inscription of the Axe (44133, and the 50335
copy), of the Crag (44134, 50336), of the Pinnacle (44136, 50337) and of the Storm (44135,
50338). Each has RequiredLevel 80 in ItemSparse, so a level-19 character cannot use the item;
the Heirloom article records that these are the heirloom versions and are applied by a level-80
character to the account-bound shoulders, which the 19 then wears. A companion that manages a
twink account therefore needs a level-80 character on the same account for the shoulder slot,
and nothing else.
Leg armour kits are ordinary consumables rather than enchants. Only the two cheapest are legal
at 19: Light Armor Kit (2304, required level 1) and Medium Armor Kit (2313, required level 5).
Heavy Armor Kit (4265) requires level 20 and every larger kit goes up from there, so a 19 stops
at the Medium kit.

7.9 Enchants: the complete set that a level 19 can use
------------------------------------------------------
Every table below is generated from SpellItemEnchantment joined to the scroll item that applies
it, filtered on the three gates in 7.2 (scroll required level 0, enchant MinLevel 0, enchant
item-level figure 1), with profession-skill enchants and the hearthstone-cast bracer family
removed because they do nothing in a battleground. Enchants whose effect is a proc or an equip
spell are listed first in each table, then the stat enchants in descending order of the figure
the client stores for them.
The Effect column is the enchant's own effect row: a stat effect prints the stat and the
EffectPointsMin figure, a proc or equip effect prints the spell name and spell id. A row ending
in 'scales' carries an EffectScalingPoints coefficient, which means the client recomputes the
figure from the target item's level at run time; the figure printed is the one stored in the
table, so enchants can be compared with each other but not read as the number a level-19
character sees. Rows without 'scales' are fixed.

7.12 Acquisition
----------------
Where an item comes from is not client data. Drop and vendor sources live in the server's world
database (creature_loot_template, gameobject_loot_template, npc_vendor, quest_template), which
is not public for 8.3.7 and was not available for this pass. ItemSparse carries the item, its
item level, its required level and its binding, and nothing about where it drops. Every
acquisition line for every item above is therefore UNVERIFIED, and a private realm's own
database is the only authority on whether a given item is obtainable there at all. Heirlooms are
the exception in practice rather than in data: they are bought from the heirloom vendors
recorded in Heirloom.db2's SourceText column, which the client does carry.

7.13 What is still unverified in this section
---------------------------------------------
  - Scaled values. Every Mists, Warlords, Legion and BfA enchant in these tables stores its top-
    of-band figure. The figure the client will show on a level-19 item is computed at run time
    from a scaling curve that is not in the tables read here. This affects the 'Breath of' and
    'Gift of' neck and ring families, the Wrath and later glove, bracer and cloak stat enchants,
    and every dummy-aura weapon proc.
  - Per-enchant item-level refusal. Gate three is read from the client's
    SpellItemEnchantment.ItemLevel field; the client-side refusal message itself is not in any
    table here, and TrinityCore does not enforce it, so a private realm may allow more than the
    client would.
  - Ring enchant profession restriction. The Heirloom article states that profession-specific
    enchantments still require the profession; the client tables carry no skill requirement on
    the ring scrolls themselves.
  - Shoulder inscriptions. The four Hodir items carry RequiredLevel 80, so they cannot be
    applied by a 19; the applying-character rule is from the Heirloom article, not from the item
    table.
  - Stat amounts on gear. ItemSparse records which stats an item carries and not how much; the
    amounts are computed from item level and quality.
  - Best-in-slot per class. The tables in 7.4 to 7.8 rank by item level and record stat types,
    and 7.9 gives the enchant answer per role. A stat-weighted best-in-slot list would need the
    stat amounts, which are not in these tables.
  - Level-scaling items with a low required level. ItemSparse holds several thousand items with
    RequiredLevel 0, 1 or 10 and an item level from 30 to 400: BfA quest rewards, the 8.3
    Alterac Valley rewards at item level 350, the class-trial sets named Recruit's, Rookie's,
    Greenhorn's, Front-Line and Auxiliary's at item levels 43 and 58, and oddities such as
    Chilton Wand (12468, item level 53, required level 1). Their real level gate is not in
    ItemSparse, so they are excluded from the rankings above and left UNVERIFIED. Where a realm
    hands one out at its raw item level it beats everything in 7.4 to 7.8, and an item at item
    level 30 or more also clears the tier-30 gate in 7.2 for Wrath scrolls.


8. UNVERIFIED
=============

Each line is a claim a careful author would want checked before relying on it, with the reason
it is open.
  - Warsong Gulch and Twin Peaks waypoint coordinates. AreaPOI has no rows on maps 489, 2106 or
    726, the 8.3.7 scripts spawn the flags from the world database, and WorldSafeLocs.db2 is not
    published for build 35662 by any mirror that answered.
  - Silvershard Mines graveyard and depot coordinates. Same reason: no AreaPOI on map 727, no
    8.3.7 script, and the 12.x script carries only the three cart spawns.
  - Seething Shore azerite node coordinates. Nodes are chosen at runtime; no static table lists
    them.
  - Behsten and Slahtz creature entries. The 8.3.7 creature table available here does not
    contain either NPC.
  - Item acquisition, for every item in section 7. Drop, vendor and quest sources live in the
    server world database, not in any client table read here.
  - How large a scaled enchant becomes on a level-19 item. Section 7.2 reads the client's three
    enchant gates and 7.9 lists every scroll that passes all three, but the client stores the
    top-of-band figure and computes the level-19 figure at run time from a curve that is not in
    these tables.
  - Best-in-slot per class. Section 7 records each item's stat types and every legal enchant,
    but a stat-weighted best-in-slot list also needs the stat amounts, which ItemSparse does not
    carry.
  - Required-level-0 items at item level 30. Items such as Vishas' Hood 66014 or Master Hunter's
    Rifle 17687 are equippable at 19 by level, but their effective item level at 19 depends on
    level scaling that is not in these tables.
  - Dual-wield and off-hand eligibility per class. A class property, not an item property; not
    read here.
  - Ring enchant profession rule. The Heirloom article says profession-specific enchantments
    still require the profession; no skill requirement is stored on the ring scrolls.
  - Shoulder inscriptions at 19. The four Sons of Hodir items carry required level 80, so a 19
    cannot apply them; the level-80-alt route is the Heirloom article's rule, not item data.
  - Alterac Valley reinforcement pool. The wiki article body says 700, its own patch list works
    out to 600, and the 8.3.7 core constant is 600. Section 4.1 prints all three; the realm's
    own constant decides.
  - Alterac Valley marshals and warmasters. The live +25% stacking buff is documented on the
    wiki, but the 8.3.7 core only spawns and deletes the guards.
  - Refinery and Quarry reinforcement interval. The 8.3.7 source itself comments the 45-second
    interval as unconfirmed.
  - Deepwind Gorge 2245 capture rules. The client proves the map, its five pins and its
    scoreboard columns, but no 8.3.7 script implements it and the 2020 article describes the
    three-mine map only.
  - Ashran objective mechanics. Only the client POI set and the scoreboard stat are verified;
    the artifact and general mechanics were out of scope for this pass.
  - Eye of the Storm base columns on map 968. The client lists Bases Assaulted (391) and Bases
    Defended (392) for the rated map, but the 8.3.7 Eye of the Storm score class carries only
    flag captures, so those two columns read zero on such a realm. The Flag Captures column (183
    on 566, 393 on 968) is tracked and fills normally.
  - Twin Peaks match clock. The 2020 article does not state a time limit and no client field
    carries one.
