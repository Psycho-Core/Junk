TWINK29 - PROFESSIONS - STEP 09 - COOKING AND FIRST AID
Server: private Battle for Azeroth 8.3.7 build 35662, TrinityCore base.
Scope: what Cooking and First Aid give a level 29 battleground twink.

1. THE FIRST THING THAT HAS TO BE SAID
   Half of this step is a removal notice. First Aid was deleted from the
   game in patch 8.0.1, the Battle for Azeroth pre-patch, which is the
   same build family as 8.3.7. Bandage crafting moved to Tailoring and
   the anti-venoms moved to Alchemy, and the trainers were renamed
   "Bandage Trainer". There is no First Aid skill to level on a 8.3.7
   character, so the question is not which bandages a 29 can make but
   which ones he can pick up.
   Cooking survives, as a secondary profession on skill line 185, and
   it caps at 225 for a 29 for a documented reason: the artisan rank is
   not sold by a trainer at all, it comes from the "Clamlette
   Surprise" quest (quest 6610, Dirge Quikcleave in Gadgetzan, Tanaris),
   which needs level 35 and 225 skill. So 225 is a hard ceiling at 29,
   and it is the cleanest ceiling in this whole set.
   The short version of both halves: Cooking is worth about +8 Stamina
   and +8 Spirit to a 29, and First Aid is worth a stack of bandages
   bought from the auction house. Neither is a reason to spend a
   profession slot.

2. FIRST AID - WHAT REPLACED IT, AND WHAT A 29 CAN CARRY
   In 8.3.7 every classic bandage is a "Classic Tailoring" recipe
   (profession tier spell 2540) and the items themselves no longer ask
   for First Aid skill. The live database shows the rework plainly:

   Item   Bandage                  Heals (8 s)  Made at
   6450   Silk Bandage                800       7928,  Tailoring 180
   6451   Heavy Silk Bandage         1280       7929,  Tailoring 210
   8544   Mageweave Bandage          1600       10840, Tailoring 240
   14529  Runecloth Bandage          1200       18629, Tailoring 290
   14530  Heavy Runecloth Bandage    1360       18630, Tailoring 300
   The heal figures are the current live values, not the 3.x ones. In 3.x
   both silk crafts were First Aid, not Tailoring: spell 7928 made Silk
   Bandage from 1x Silk Cloth 4306 at First Aid 150, difficulty
   150/150/180/210, and the item healed 400; spell 7929 made Heavy Silk
   Bandage from 2x Silk Cloth at First Aid 180, difficulty 180/180/210/240,
   and the item healed 640. Heavy Runecloth healed 2000 in 3.x against
   1360 live. So the rework did not move every row the same way, and live
   Mageweave out-heals live Runecloth, which is not what a 3.x player
   expects. Re-check one in game before trusting the ordering.
   The practical consequences for a 29:
   - Using a bandage needs no profession and no character level any
     more; the item tooltip in the live database has no requirement
     line at all. A 29 can use a Heavy Runecloth Bandage he bought.
   - Making one is a Tailoring job. The silk tier is inside a 29's 225
     ceiling (Silk Bandage 6450 at 180, Heavy Silk Bandage 6451 at 210,
     1280 healing) and everything better starts at Mageweave 8544 at 240,
     which a 29 cannot reach. So a 29 tailor's best bandage is the heavy
     silk one, bought or made.
   - Bandages share one cooldown and apply a 60 second "Recently
     Bandaged" debuff on the user, and they are flagged usable in
     arenas. That makes them a real battleground item: one free 1200 to
     1600 heal per minute, for anyone, at level 29, for a few silver on
     the auction house.
   - Anti-venoms are Alchemy recipes now, not first aid. See step 02.
   Note for the set: step 05 (Tailoring) was written before this was
   found and does not list bandages. They belong in the Tailoring file
   whenever that file is revised.
   Table the shopping list, not the profession: buy 20 Mageweave
   Bandages 8544 if they are cheap - they heal 1600 for no profession at
   all - or make Heavy Silk Bandages 6451 at 210 if your twink happens to
   be a tailor anyway, and spend the profession slot on gear.

3. COOKING AT 29 - THE HONEST ANSWER
   Cooked food gives a "well fed" buff only if you eat for at least ten
   seconds while seated, and the buff lasts 15 minutes. The foods that
   carry a primary stat are the ones twinks want, and every one of them
   is level-gated above 29. The foods a 29 can actually eat give
   Stamina and Spirit:

   5479  Crispy Lizard Tail - item level 22, requires level 12.
         Restores 552 health over 24 sec; well fed for 10 sec gives
         +6 Stamina and +6 Spirit for 15 minutes.
         Spell 6418, Cooking 100, difficulty 100/140/160/180, 1x Thunder
         Lizard Tail 5470, needs a cooking fire. Recipe 5488 is sold by
         Tari'qa (npc 3482) in the Barrens.
   12209 Lean Wolf Steak - item level 25, requires level 15.
         Restores 552 health over 24 sec; +6 Stamina and +6 Spirit for
         15 minutes. Spell 15853, Cooking 125, difficulty 125/165/185/205.
   12210 Roast Raptor - item level 35, requires level 25.
         Restores 874.8 health over 27 sec; well fed for 10 sec gives
         +8 Stamina and +8 Spirit for 15 minutes.
         Spell 15855, Cooking 175, difficulty 175/215/235/255,
         1x Raptor Flesh 12184. Recipe 12228 from Hammon Karwn or Keena
         in the Arathi Highlands.
   3729 Soothing Turtle Bisque - item level 35, requires level 25; the
         same level-25 band as Roast Raptor, buff magnitude unverified.
   So the ladder inside a 29's reach is +6 at skill 100, +6 again at 125,
   and +8 at 175. Level Cooking to 175 and cook Roast Raptor; that is the
   best food buff a 29 can eat, eight stamina and eight spirit for
   fifteen minutes.
   That is the whole combat value of the profession at 29 - eight
   stamina and eight spirit for fifteen minutes. For a companion
   character this is still worth modelling, because it is a buff a
   non-caster can hand out for almost nothing.

4. THE BUFF FOOD A 29 CANNOT EAT - ALL ALT
   Level requirements are the gate, not cooking skill. The profession
   list sorts out like this, and every entry below was read off its
   item page:
   17222 Spider Sausage - requires 35.
   13932 Poached Sunscale Salmon - requires 35.
   6887  Spotted Yellowtail - requires 35.
   12216 Spiced Chili Crab - requires 40.
   18254 Runn Tum Tuber Surprise - requires 45; +10 Intellect for
         10 minutes; spell 22761, Cooking 275.
   20452 Smoked Desert Dumplings - requires 45; well fed gives +20
         Strength for 15 minutes; spell 24801, Cooking 285.
   35563 Charred Bear Kabobs - requires 45.
   So the Strength, Intellect and the biggest Stamina foods are all 15 or
   30 levels away. Cooking at 29 tops out at +8/+8 from Roast Raptor; the
   only reason to push past 175 is to cook for other people.

5. TRAINERS AND THE FOUR RANKS
   The rank gates are level gates as well as skill gates, and the first
   three are open to a 29:
   apprentice - level 5, trainer taught.
   journeyman - level 10 and Cooking 50, trainer taught.
   expert - level 20 and Cooking 125. Not trainer taught: it is the
     Expert Cookbook 16072, sold by Wulan at Shadowprey Village in
     Desolace (Horde) and by Shandrina in Ashenvale (Alliance).
   artisan - level 35 and Cooking 225, "Clamlette Surprise" quest 6610
     from Dirge Quikcleave in Gadgetzan, Tanaris. Needs 12x Giant Egg
     12207, 10x Zesty Clam Meat 7974 and 20x Alterac Swiss 8932. ALT.
   Cooking trainers, from the classic trainer list (creature entries not
   read for this file, so all of them are unverified):
   Horde - Zamja in Orgrimmar, Eunice Burch in Undercity, Aska
     Mistrunner in Thunder Bluff, Pyall Silentstride in Mulgore, Duhng
     in the Barrens, Mudduk in Stranglethorn Vale.
   Alliance - Stephen Ryback in Stormwind, Daryl Riknussun in Ironforge,
     Alegorn in Darnassus, Gremlock Pilsnor in Dun Morogh, Tomas in
     Elwynn Forest, Zarrin in Teldrassil.
   Every recipe in the route below except two is trainer-taught. The
   exceptions are Curiously Tasty Omelet (Kendor Kabonka in Stormwind,
   Keena in the Arathi Highlands) and Roast Raptor (Hammon Karwn in the
   Arathi Highlands, Keena again).
   A cooking fire is required: the first recipe in the route carries
   "Requires Cooking Fire", and Basic Campfire 818 is the Cooking and
   Survival ability that drops one. In a capital you can cook at any
   brazier; in the field you carry the campfire.

   That is about 250 combines and 250 pieces of meat, fish or egg, one
   vendor water and nothing else. Two alternatives worth knowing: at
   175-225 Soothing Turtle Bisque 3400 uses Turtle Meat 3712 and its
   recipe is a quest reward from Chef Jessen or Christoph Jeffcoat in
   the Hillsbrad Foothills, and Mithril Headed Trout 20916 uses Raw
   Mithril Head Trout 8365.
   For a twink the honest stop is 175, not 225. Skill 100 buys Crispy
   Lizard Tail at +6 Stamina and Spirit, 125 buys Lean Wolf Steak for the
   same buff, and 175 buys Roast Raptor 12210 at +8 and +8 - the last
   food upgrade a 29 can use. Everything from 175 to 225 cooks food a 29
   is not allowed to eat.
   One warning about the first row: wowhead's own difficulty line for
   Charred Wolf Meat reads 45/65/85, while the classic route cooks it
   from skill 1. The route is what players actually did, but if your
   core enforces 45, cook whatever the trainer allows until that opens.
   Vendor reagents: Soothing Spices 3713, Hot Spices 2692 and
   Refreshing Spring Water 159 come from the cooking supply vendors next
   to the trainers. Do not buy them on the auction house.

7. ALT - WHAT A 29 CANNOT DO
   35563 Charred Bear Kabobs, 12216 Spiced Chili Crab, 17222 Spider
     Sausage, 13932 Poached Sunscale Salmon, 6887 Spotted Yellowtail -
     level 35 and 40 requirements.
   18254 Runn Tum Tuber Surprise and 20452 Smoked Desert Dumplings -
     level 45, and the dumplings are the +20 Strength food.
   Artisan Cooking - the Clamlette Surprise quest needs level 35.
   Everything in the Outland, Northrend and Battle for Azeroth cooking
     tiers, including feasts and the BfA "Kul Tiran and Zandalari
     Cooking" recipes - those need level 110 and a separate skill bar.
   Any bandage above the silk tier as a crafting project: Mageweave at
     240, Runecloth at 290 and Heavy Runecloth at 300, all past the 225
     ceiling. Buying them is fine and is the whole point of section 2.
   Anti-venoms - they left First Aid for Alchemy in 8.0.1.
   A higher-level cook can make the two foods a 29 can eat, and both are
     tradeable, so cooking at 29 is optional in a way bandages are not.

8. WHICH CLASSES SHOULD TAKE IT
   1. Nobody, on combat grounds. Cooking gives +8 Stamina and Spirit,
      First Aid gives a 1200-1600 heal per minute that anyone can use
      from a vendor stack. Neither is worth one of the two profession
      slots against Engineering or Blacksmithing.
   2. A companion character meant to support a group. Cooking is worth
      exactly one thing here: a 29 cook can hand a +8 Stamina and
      Spirit food buff (Roast Raptor 12210) to the party, which costs a
      stack of food and no skill to receive. Model it as a one-line buff,
      not a profession.
   3. A twink with a free slot and no gold problem. Take Cooking to 175
      for the food buff and stop; take Tailoring only if the same
      character wants bags and cloth, and buy his bandages rather than
      make them.
   4. Everyone: carry bandages. Mageweave Bandage 8544 at 1600 healing
      over 8 seconds, one per minute, usable in arenas, no level or
      profession requirement, is the cheapest survivability a 29 can
      buy, and it does not cost a profession slot at all.
   Last line: First Aid does not exist at 8.3.7, Cooking does and gives
   +8 Stamina and +8 Spirit at skill 175. Level Cooking to 175, buy or
   make heavy silk bandages, and spend the real profession slots on the
   files that give gear.
